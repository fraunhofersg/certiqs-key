## Module summary

Summaries explain purpose and content. They are non-normative; the claim statements, proofs and declared assumptions remain authoritative.

**Non-normative summary.** This module compiles a protocol specification into the finite protocol declaration the key\-rate engine consumes\. The specification has five parts: Alice's source as the transmitter module emits it, the receiver's measurement, a reduction chain in two segments \(source links that replace Alice's signal ensemble, then receiver links that replace Bob's measurement\), the public announcements with sifting and key rule, and the constraint entries; the declaration comes with an index map fixing the order of every alphabet and of the sectors\. Operators on the measured system are lists of sector blocks against a declared sector map, every numerical condition is decided exactly on the declared binary64 numbers at no tolerance, and the compiler passes declared data through unchanged, constructing only the raw\-key map, the index map and the measurement families that carry the weights of public choices\. Its main results are three\. A public choice of known bias, such as a basis choice, enters as a weight inside the measurement operators and never as a separate fraction: the weighted family stays a measurement, a weight that depends on no private setting is reproduced exactly as the probability of its choice value wherever the weighted family is the one emitted, and a weight that depends on the private setting of a measuring station, of either party, is applied after that station's reduction chain and commutes with it \([Theorem “Encoding of public choices in the measurement operators”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:choice-encoding)\)\. Passing the validator's eight checks establishes every target condition of a well\-formed declaration, and every check except the feasibility check is complete, so a well\-formed declaration is refused only when neither a default candidate nor a supplied witness state is verified to be feasible \([Theorem “Soundness, completeness and decidability of the validator”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:validator-soundness)\)\. Gates at emission decide whether a well\-formed declaration may back a key\-length claim, and every gate failure has one fixed outcome: a refusal, or an emission flagged as not eligible to back such a claim \([Theorem “The emission gates and their outcomes”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:gates)\)\. The compiler decides what the record of the reduction chain and the transmitter's output make decidable and names the rest: the soundness of each reduction link, the licence that restricts the attacks considered, and the physical fidelity of the declared data are obligations it records and never establishes\.

### 1 The protocol declaration

[Dependency view](group-01-the-protocol-declaration.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The object the compiler emits is a protocol declaration: finite systems, a sector map, Alice's marginal slot, the two measurement families, the public maps, the raw\-key map and a typed constraint list, every operator on the measured system being given as the list of its sector blocks\. In its general format the sector map is an ordered list of factors, Alice's system may carry sectors, each measured factor declares its own family and no entry of their product is declared, Alice's measurement may be declared in ensemble form, as positive blocks summing to her marginal, and the public discussion is a transcript of messages\. Declared operator data are exactly admissible when five conditions hold of the declared numbers, one of them that every binary64 addition forming a completeness sum is exact, and the eight target conditions state when a declaration, with the index map that orders its alphabets and sectors, is well\-formed\. The consuming engine decides the same five conditions, and the declarations admitted here are a subset of those it admits \([Remark “Relation to the consuming engine's admission conditions”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:engine-admission)\)\.

### 2 Feasible states and licensed constraints

[Dependency view](group-02-feasible-states-and-licensed-constraints.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The feasible set holds the normalised positive block\-list states that meet every constraint entry and, in trusted\-source mode, the declared marginal, each condition read exactly; Hermiticity of the constraint operators and of the state, with that of the declared right\-hand sides, is what makes the two sides of each constraint condition comparable \([Lemma “Hermiticity of the constraint values”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/lem:hermitian-constraint-values)\)\. Restricting the states to sector\-block\-diagonal ones is a restriction in the unsafe direction that the operator type carries and the consuming engine proves sound \([Remark “Restriction of the feasible set to block\-diagonal states”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:block-restriction)\)\. A constraint list is licensed for its source mode when every entry is declared in the form the mode admits or carries the party\-local tag, and licensing reads the declared tag and compares no numbers\. A party\-local entry, whose condition holds against the state's marginal on the systems other than its own factor, is sound only under a certificate owed by the producer of the reduction chain, which the attack\-set licence of the list attests \([Remark “Soundness of a party\-local entry”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:party-local-soundness)\)\. The example writes out a declaration for an untrusted source serving two measuring parties, with a party\-local entry\.

### 4 Public choices as measurement weights

[Dependency view](group-04-public-choices-as-measurement-weights.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A public choice of known bias enters the declaration as a weight multiplying a complete conditional measurement, either independently of every setting or, at a measured factor of either party after its chain, through a stochastic matrix read off the private setting of the station that measures it\. The main result, [Theorem “Encoding of public choices in the measurement operators”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:choice-encoding), shows that the encoding keeps the family a measurement, that the setting\-independent form reproduces the declared weights exactly wherever it is the emitted family, that the setting\-dependent relabelling commutes with the reduction chain, and that no separate fixed fraction is ever emitted\. Each declared conditional family is decided before any product is formed; the remarks and the example say why that decision cannot be left to the validator, where the relabelling is applied, and why the outcome alphabet is chosen upstream\. A factor enclosure of a delivered block survives the setting\-dependent weighting, the weight going into its core \([Lemma “A factor enclosure through a setting\-dependent weighting”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/lem:weighted-factor)\)\.

- 4.1 [`protocol-compiler/def:weighted`](../claims/def-weighted.tex): Choice-weighted families ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/def:weighted))

  **Non-normative summary.** A public choice enters as a weight on a complete conditional measurement: one declared weight per choice value, or, at a measured factor after its chain, a stochastic weight read through its station's setting\. Weights and families are binary64 numbers read exactly; products are formed exactly\.

- 4.2 [`protocol-compiler/lem:weighted`](../claims/lem-weighted.tex): Properties of the choice-weighted families ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/lem:weighted))

  **Non-normative summary.** Weighting keeps a measurement a measurement and acts block by block\. The independent form has the declared state\-independent choice marginal; the setting\-dependent form returns the delivered family when the choice is forgotten, and its choice marginal may depend on the state through the setting\.

- 4.3 [`protocol-compiler/lem:weighted-factor`](../claims/lem-weighted-factor.tex): A factor enclosure through a setting-dependent weighting ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/lem:weighted-factor))

  **Non-normative summary.** Weighting a delivered element by its positive stochastic weight keeps a factor enclosure of each of its blocks: the same box, the core multiplied by the weight\. A pair outside the support of the weighting is no outcome and carries no block\.

- 4.4 [`protocol-compiler/thm:choice-encoding`](../claims/thm-choice-encoding.tex): Encoding of public choices in the measurement operators ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:choice-encoding))

  **Non-normative summary.** A public choice of known bias enters as weights inside the measurement operators: the family stays a POVM, fixed weights are reproduced exactly as choice probabilities where the weighted family is emitted, and setting\-dependent weights commute with the chain\. A fraction outside them is refused\.

- 4.5 [`protocol-compiler/rem:exactness`](../claims/rem-exactness.tex): Scope of the exact encoding ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:exactness))

  **Non-normative summary.** The exact choice marginal fixes the sum over outcomes, not the individual probabilities, so no state\-independent weight can be factored out of a security argument\. Nor does it establish D\(iii\) of the emitted binary64 family, which check V3 decides after the emission decisions\.

- 4.6 [`protocol-compiler/rem:condfamily`](../claims/rem-condfamily.tex): Deciding the conditional families ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:condfamily))

  **Non-normative summary.** Each conditional family is decided by the compilation map, not the validator, since the emitted family's conditions do not imply it; the converse gives \(T\), \(H\), \(P\) and, with \(S\), \(C\)\. For a choice\-weighted physical family, only its own \(S\) is left to decide on the family itself\.

- 4.7 [`protocol-compiler/ex:condfamily`](../claims/ex-condfamily.tex): An exact weighted family built from incomplete conditional families ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/ex:condfamily))

  **Non-normative summary.** Two weights of one half and two one\-element conditional families that are not POVMs give a weighted family passing checks V1 and V3 exactly\. The emitted conditions therefore do not imply the per\-choice ones, and the declared weight is then not the probability the declaration implements\.

- 4.8 [`protocol-compiler/rem:relabel`](../claims/rem-relabel.tex): The setting-dependent relabelling after the chain ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:relabel))

  **Non-normative summary.** A choice whose bias depends on a station's setting is applied after the chain, to both sides of the last link's relation, by one stochastic matrix; by linearity the relation keeps the same channel\. It is not a link, and it is read through the setting alone\.

- 4.9 [`protocol-compiler/rem:alphabet`](../claims/rem-alphabet.tex): The outcome alphabet is chosen upstream ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:alphabet))

  **Non-normative summary.** Bob's emitted outcome alphabet is chosen upstream, the receiver's declared coarse\-graining deciding how much of the click pattern survives\. Every object of this module indexed by it is stated over any finite alphabet, so a fine\-grained instance changes declared data and no claim here\.


### 5 Sifting, key computability and feasibility

[Dependency view](group-05-sifting-key-computability-and-feasibility.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Each announcement is a function of its sender's readout and of the announcements before it, sifting is determined by the announcements, and the holder computes the raw key locally, for a transcript of messages as for two announcement maps \([Theorem “Announcement locality, sifting and party computability”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:sifting-and-key)\)\. Feasibility is the one target condition that is not a typing or a finite syntactic check: an empty constraint list has an explicit product state, and otherwise the compiler verifies a default candidate and, if it fails, a supplied witness, and refuses when neither verifies \([Theorem “Feasibility by an explicit or a verified candidate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:feasibility)\)\.

- 5.1 [`protocol-compiler/thm:sifting-and-key`](../claims/thm-sifting-and-key.tex): Announcement locality, sifting and party computability ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:sifting-and-key))

  **Non-normative summary.** Each announcement is a function of its sender's readout and of the announcements before it, sifting is determined by the announcements, and the holder computes the raw key locally\. An ensemble\-form measurement slot of Alice's denotes a POVM\.

- 5.2 [`protocol-compiler/rem:sidecond`](../claims/rem-sidecond.tex): Necessity of the key-rule side condition ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:sidecond))

  **Non-normative summary.** Without the condition that the key rule map into the key values, the specification's sifting predicate stops witnessing announcement\-determined sifting\. Check V5 catches only a key rule that withholds a key on part of a passing class, so the condition is a clause of [A\-SPEC](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/ass:spec)\.

- 5.3 [`protocol-compiler/rem:vacuous`](../claims/rem-vacuous.tex): Party computability read against the readouts ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:vacuous))

  **Non-normative summary.** Read against the joint outcome pair, party computability would hold for every raw\-key map; read against the holder's readout it is a genuine restriction\. It matters most where both parties' data share one outcome symbol, as when no source is trusted\.

- 5.4 [`protocol-compiler/ex:transcript`](../claims/ex-transcript.tex): A three-message transcript ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/ex:transcript))

  **Non-normative summary.** Alice announces the kind of round, Bob discloses his bit only on test rounds, and Alice then discloses hers only on detected test rounds\. Party computability holds by the signatures of the tables, and two independent announcement maps cannot express the protocol\.

- 5.5 [`protocol-compiler/thm:feasibility`](../claims/thm-feasibility.tex): Feasibility by an explicit or a verified candidate ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:feasibility))

  **Non-normative summary.** An empty constraint list has an explicit product state in the feasible set\. Otherwise the compiler verifies, exactly and block by block, the default candidate and, if it fails, a supplied witness, and refuses when neither verifies\.

- 5.6 [`protocol-compiler/rem:nosearch`](../claims/rem-nosearch.tex): Feasibility by witness rather than by search ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:nosearch))

  **Non-normative summary.** Deciding non\-emptiness of the feasible set for a general list is a semidefinite feasibility problem, while whoever produced the entries can exhibit a reachable state\. A supplied witness turns D\(vii\) into a check, and refusing when no candidate verifies avoids an infimum over the empty set\.


### 6 The emitted validator

[Dependency view](group-06-the-emitted-validator.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The validator's eight checks are decided exactly on the candidate declaration\. The main result, [Theorem “Soundness, completeness and decidability of the validator”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:validator-soundness), shows that passing every check establishes all eight target conditions of the declared numbers, that every check except the feasibility check is complete, so that a well\-formed declaration can be refused only for want of a verified witness, and that every decision is finite, exact and taken block by block\. Such a refusal means that no verified feasible state was shown, not that the protocol is defective\.

- 6.1 [`protocol-compiler/def:validator`](../claims/def-validator.tex): The emitted validator ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/def:validator))

  **Non-normative summary.** Eight exact checks on a candidate declaration: sector maps, the marginal, the operators, the key alphabet, sifting, constraint typing, feasibility and party computability\. Feasibility passes only on an empty list, a verified default candidate or a verified supplied witness\.

- 6.2 [`protocol-compiler/thm:validator-soundness`](../claims/thm-validator-soundness.tex): Soundness, completeness and decidability of the validator ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:validator-soundness))

  **Non-normative summary.** Passing the validator's eight checks establishes every target condition of a well\-formed declaration, and every check but the feasibility check is complete, so a well\-formed declaration is refused only when no default or supplied candidate is verified feasible\. Every decision is finite and exact\.

- 6.3 [`protocol-compiler/rem:completeness`](../claims/rem-completeness.tex): The two directions of the validator ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/rem:completeness))

  **Non-normative summary.** Soundness keeps an ill\-formed declaration from being emitted; completeness, which holds for every target condition but feasibility, makes a refusal name a real defect\. A refusal at V7 means only that no verified feasible state was shown\.


### 7 Consuming the reduction chain

### 9 The emission gates

[Dependency view](group-09-the-emission-gates.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A well\-formed declaration may back a key\-length claim only when every check passes, the transmitter's source\-replacement licence record is present and re\-decided wherever the marginal slot is filled and Alice's dimension is at least two, and a non\-empty constraint list is licensed and carries an attack\-set licence\. The main result, [Theorem “The emission gates and their outcomes”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/thm:gates), shows that every gate is decidable or a presence test and that every gate failure has one fixed outcome, a refusal or an emission that is not key\-length\-eligible; passing a gate asserts nothing about the apparatus or about attack\-set inclusion\. Dropping a trusted marginal, and reading homogeneous constraints as scalars, enlarges the feasible set and so can only lower a minimised quantity \([Proposition “Dropping a trusted marginal enlarges the feasible set”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/prop:conservative)\); no reachable row of the gate outcome table invokes it\.

**Non-normative summary.** The engine emits the eligibility flag true only for a key\-length\-eligible declaration and produces, for each gate failure, the outcome the gate outcome table fixes\. It tests attestations for presence and never for truth, and records a vacuous pass of the licensed\-class gate as vacuous\.

- 14.1 [`protocol-compiler/req:eligibility-flag`](../claims/req-eligibility-flag.tex): The key-length eligibility flag ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/req:eligibility-flag))

  **Non-normative summary.** The emitted flag reads true only for a declaration that passes all three gates of [Definition “Key\-length eligibility”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/def:eligible)\. A declaration marked false may be explored numerically but backs no key\-length claim\.

- 14.2 [`protocol-compiler/req:gate-outcomes`](../claims/req-gate-outcomes.tex): Outcome of each gate failure ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/req:gate-outcomes))

  **Non-normative summary.** A failing check or a failing certificate is refused with the check or the condition named, whatever else fails; an unlicensed list or an absent attack\-set licence alone is emitted marked not key\-length\-eligible\. The engine never supplies a missing attestation or proceeds as though it had one\.

- 14.3 [`protocol-compiler/req:attestation-presence`](../claims/req-attestation-presence.tex): Attestations tested for presence only ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/req:attestation-presence))

  **Non-normative summary.** The retention attestation \(SR3\) behind gate \(G2\) and the attack\-set licence behind gate \(G3\)\(b\) are tested for presence and never for truth\. The engine never supplies one and never verifies one\.

- 14.4 [`protocol-compiler/req:vacuous-gate-report`](../claims/req-vacuous-gate-report.tex): A vacuous pass recorded as vacuous ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/req:vacuous-gate-report))

  **Non-normative summary.** In untrusted\-source mode gate \(G3\)\(a\) passes by construction, so the gate report records it as passed vacuously and not as passed\. A vacuous pass is never reported as evidence of anything\.
