A snap moves a measurement family onto a lattice
(Definition~\ref{def:snap}), and nothing established relates a moved block
to the model it replaced. A run that holds a \tref{reference} of the family
and evaluates the numbers its key length rests on with that reference in
place of the snap reads the snapped family only to find the points at which
it evaluates them; on such a block, \tref{evaluated off its snap}, no premise
about the snap is needed, and this assumption does not cover it. Where the
run does read a moved block, because no reference reaches the evaluation, the
evaluation on the reference does not count, or the key length is formed from
a number certified on the snapped family in its place, the analysis of the
run is
there an analysis of the snapped family, a block whose data are an evaluation
of a model not known exactly on it included, even where the snap leaves that
evaluation as it stands. This assumption is the premise under which that
analysis describes the device. Where the snap had to give an element support
that the data deny, a block the record flags, the premise is stronger, since
the snapped family may treat as possible an outcome that the model treats as
impossible. On the blocks it covers it takes the place of the model as a
description of the device, so that the two are never asserted of the same
block; for a family that a producer snaps in its own emission, which is the
target of a reduction and not the measurement of a device, it is the premise
that what the run concludes from the emission holds of the snapped family. A
run none of whose blocks it covers does not invoke it.

\begin{assumption}\label{ass:snapped-measurement}
Let $F$ be a snap (Definition~\ref{def:snap}) of an operator family $D$ read
as a measurement, against a \tref{sector map} with blocks $s\in\mathsf{S}$
(Definition~\ref{def:sector-map}), over an alphabet $J$, with $D$ the data the
snap is made from: the data as an instance states them, or, where the
instance computes those data from a model of the family, that model, a
\tref{reference} of the family (Definition~\ref{def:exact-reference}), or a
producer's exact construction of what it emits
(Definition~\ref{def:declared-data}). Let $B\subseteq\mathsf{S}$ be the set of
blocks that the record of the run names as resting on this assumption: the
\tref{moved} blocks of $F$ (Definition~\ref{def:snap}), those on which the
record does not show, by an exact decision, that $F^{(s)}_j=D^{(s)}_j$ for
every $j\in J$, that are not \tref{evaluated off its snap}
(Definition~\ref{def:evaluated-off-snap}). A \tref{kept} block and a block
evaluated off its snap do not belong to $B$. Every other moved block belongs
to $B$, whatever its cause; among them are the blocks on which the snap gives
an element support outside the \tref{structural support} of $D^{(s)}_j$
(Definition~\ref{def:snap}).
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.4em]
\item Where $D$ is the data an instance states as the measurement of a
  device, the model it computes them from, or a reference of it, then on every block $s\in B$ the block on $s$
  of the measurement the device performs, read against the sector map, is
  the family $\{F^{(s)}_j\}_{j\in J}$ with the entries recorded for the snap.
  On such a block, any statement of another assumption of the run that a
  model describes that measurement is not asserted, and this clause holds in
  its place.
\item Where $D$ is a producer's exact construction of what it emits, every
  conclusion about the device that the claims of the run draw from the
  emitted family, under their hypothesis that it is the producer's exact
  construction, holds when the emitted family is $F^{(s)}$ on every block
  $s\in B$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{assumption}
