An enclosure whose disc contains zero does not say whether the entry it
encloses is zero. A family evaluated at its centres would then carry, at such
an entry, a number of the order of the radius that the model may not have,
and a support decision taken on the evaluation could see support there that
the model lacks. Setting such a centre to zero, and widening the radius by
what was removed, keeps the enclosure valid; the lemma says so and fixes the
rational bound the widening uses. An enclosure formed by arithmetic on
enclosures, of a matrix known to be Hermitian, need not come with a Hermitian
centre and symmetric radii; part (iv) gives it them, so that it can be
recentred in turn.

\begin{lemma}\label{lem:recentred-enclosure}
Let $(C,r)$ be an entrywise enclosure of a complex $d\times d$ matrix $X$
(Definition~\ref{def:exact-reference}), and let
\begin{equation*}
  Z\;\coloneqq\;\big\{(i,l):(\operatorname{Re}C_{il})^{2}
     +(\operatorname{Im}C_{il})^{2}\le r_{il}^{2}\big\},
\end{equation*}
the entries whose enclosure contains $0$. Put $C'_{il}\coloneqq0$ and
$r'_{il}\coloneqq r_{il}+|\operatorname{Re}C_{il}|+|\operatorname{Im}C_{il}|$
for $(i,l)\in Z$, and $C'_{il}\coloneqq C_{il}$, $r'_{il}\coloneqq r_{il}$
otherwise. Then:
\begin{enumerate}[label=(\roman*),leftmargin=2.4em]
\item $Z$ is decided by comparisons of rationals, and $(C',r')$ is an
  entrywise enclosure of $X$: $C'$ is Hermitian with entries in
  $\mathbb{Q}(i)$, and $r'_{il}=r'_{li}\ge0$ are rationals;
\item $r'_{il}\le(1+\sqrt2)\,r_{il}$ for every $(i,l)$, and $r'_{il}=r_{il}$
  wherever $C_{il}=0$; in particular an entry that is
  \tref{structurally zero} for $(C,r)$ (Definition~\ref{def:snap}) is so
  for $(C',r')$, and every entry of $Z$ has centre $0$ in $(C',r')$;
\item if $G$ is a \tref{reference} with constant $c$ read with enclosures
  $\big(C^{(s)}_j,r^{(s)}_j\big)$, then $G$ read with the enclosures
  $\big(C'^{(s)}_j,r'^{(s)}_j\big)$ formed by (i) from each is a reference
  with the same constant; it is the same family $G$, and it is an
  \tref{exact reference} exactly when the first is;
\item if $X$ is Hermitian and $(\tilde C,\tilde r)$ is any pair of a complex
  $d\times d$ matrix $\tilde C$ with entries in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ and rationals
  $\tilde r_{il}\ge0$ with $|X_{il}-\tilde C_{il}|\le\tilde r_{il}$ for all
  $i,l$, Hermitian or symmetric or not, then
  $\big(\tfrac12(\tilde C+\tilde C^{\dagger}),\tfrac12(\tilde r+\tilde r^{\mathsf T})\big)$
  is an entrywise enclosure of $X$, equal to $(\tilde C,\tilde r)$ where
  $\tilde C$ is Hermitian and $\tilde r$ symmetric, so (i)--(ii) apply to
  it.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
(i) The real and imaginary parts of an entry of $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ are rationals
and $r_{il}$ is rational, so the inequality defining $Z$ is a comparison of
rationals, and $r'_{il}$ is a sum of rationals. For $(i,l)\in Z$,
\begin{equation*}
  |X_{il}-C'_{il}|=|X_{il}|\le|X_{il}-C_{il}|+|C_{il}|
  \le r_{il}+|\operatorname{Re}C_{il}|+|\operatorname{Im}C_{il}|=r'_{il},
\end{equation*}
by the triangle inequality, \eqref{eq:entrywise-enclosure} and
$|a+ib|\le|a|+|b|$; off $Z$ nothing changed. $C$ is Hermitian, so
$C_{li}=\overline{C_{il}}$, which has the same modulus and the same moduli of
real and imaginary part; with $r_{li}=r_{il}$ this gives
$(l,i)\in Z\iff(i,l)\in Z$ and $r'_{li}=r'_{il}$. On $Z$ both $C'_{il}$ and
$C'_{li}$ are $0=\overline{0}$, and off $Z$ they are $C_{il}$ and
$\overline{C_{il}}$, so $C'$ is Hermitian; a diagonal entry of $C$ is real
and stays real or becomes $0$.

(ii) For $(i,l)\in Z$, $|\operatorname{Re}C_{il}|+|\operatorname{Im}C_{il}|
\le\sqrt2\,|C_{il}|\le\sqrt2\,r_{il}$ by the Cauchy--Schwarz inequality in
$\mathbb{R}^{2}$ and the definition of $Z$; off $Z$, $r'=r$. Where
$C_{il}=0$ the added term is $0$. An entry is structurally zero for an
enclosure exactly when its centre and its radius are both $0$
(Definition~\ref{def:snap}); such an entry lies in $Z$, keeps centre $0$ and
radius $0$, and an entry with centre or radius not zero keeps a radius not
zero or a centre not zero, since $r'_{il}\ge r_{il}$ and $C'_{il}\neq0$ only
off $Z$, where $C'_{il}=C_{il}$. Every entry of $Z$ has centre $0$ by
construction.

(iii) Definition~\ref{def:exact-reference} asks of a reference that its
blocks be Hermitian, positive semidefinite and complete at $c$, properties of
$G$ alone, and that each block be read with an entrywise enclosure with a
Hermitian centre in $\mathbb{Q}(i)$ and symmetric non-negative rational radii,
which (i) gives. The family $G$ is unchanged. Every radius of $(C,r)$ is $0$
exactly when every radius of $(C',r')$ is: if every $r_{il}=0$, an entry lies
in $Z$ only where $C_{il}=0$, so $r'=r$ by (ii); and $r'\ge r$ always.

(iv) The entries of $\tfrac12(\tilde C+\tilde C^{\dagger})$ are
$\tfrac12(\tilde C_{il}+\overline{\tilde C_{li}})\in\mathbb{Q}(i)$, the
matrix is Hermitian, and $\tfrac12(\tilde r_{il}+\tilde r_{li})$ is a
non-negative rational, symmetric in $(i,l)$. Since $X_{il}=\overline{X_{li}}$,
\begin{equation*}
  \Big|X_{il}-\tfrac12\big(\tilde C_{il}+\overline{\tilde C_{li}}\big)\Big|
  \le\tfrac12|X_{il}-\tilde C_{il}|+\tfrac12\big|\overline{X_{li}-\tilde C_{li}}\big|
  \le\tfrac12(\tilde r_{il}+\tilde r_{li}).
\end{equation*}
Where $\tilde C$ is Hermitian and $\tilde r$ symmetric, both averages return
them unchanged.
\end{proof}
