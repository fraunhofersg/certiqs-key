## Part I: Mathematics

### 1 Quantum-information preliminaries

[Dependency view](group-01-quantum-information-preliminaries.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** These are the quantum\-information objects the entropy optimisation is written in: pinching, the sandwiched Rényi divergence with its two conditional entropies, and the classical divergences with the quantum relative entropy\. The section's main results, two lemmas, establish what the optimisation needs of them, that the extended divergence kernel is jointly convex, lower semicontinuous and non\-increasing in its second argument \([Lemma “Extended divergence kernel”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:kernel)\), and that pinching lowers the trace power \([Lemma “Pinching lowers the trace power”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:pinchmono)\)\. Two published results close the section, the pinched trace\-power form of the conditional Rényi entropy \([Fact “Pinched trace\-power form of the conditional Rényi entropy”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/fact:pinch-id)\) and the concavity of the pinched trace\-power functional \([Fact “Concavity of the pinched trace\-power functional”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/fact:concavity)\)\.

- 1.1 [`foundations/def:pinching`](../claims/def-pinching.tex): Operator conventions and pinching ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:pinching))

  **Non-normative summary.** Operator, state and channel conventions, and the pinching of a complete family of orthogonal projectors, which deletes the coherences between their ranges\. A pinching is a self\-adjoint, idempotent, unital mixture of unitary conjugations whose fixed points are the block\-diagonal operators\.

- 1.2 [`foundations/def:renyi`](../claims/def-renyi.tex): Sandwiched Rényi divergence and conditional entropies ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:renyi))

  **Non-normative summary.** The sandwiched Rényi divergence of order α \> 1 and the two conditional entropies it induces, one at the fixed marginal and one optimised over the marginal, in bits\. The fixed\-marginal entropy is no larger, so substituting it inside a lower bound is conservative, and the reverse substitution is not\.

- 1.3 [`foundations/def:kl`](../claims/def-kl.tex): Classical divergences and the quantum relative entropy ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:kl))

  **Non-normative summary.** The base\-2 Kullback–Leibler and Rényi divergences of probability vectors and the quantum relative entropy, with their conventions at zero\. The Kullback–Leibler divergence, the statistics penalty of the MEAT objective, is non\-negative and vanishes only at equal vectors\.

- 1.4 [`foundations/lem:kernel`](../claims/lem-kernel.tex): Extended divergence kernel ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:kernel))

  **Non-normative summary.** The divergence kernel d\(u, v\) = u log₂\(u/v\), extended to vanishing arguments, is jointly convex, lower semicontinuous and non\-increasing in v\. These properties keep the objective well defined at support boundaries and let it be composed with concave branch quantities\.

- 1.5 [`foundations/lem:pinchmono`](../claims/lem-pinchmono.tex): Pinching lowers the trace power ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:pinchmono))

  **Non-normative summary.** For α \> 1, pinching the α\-th root of a positive semidefinite operator and raising the result to the power α never increases the trace, and leaves it unchanged exactly when the operator is block diagonal for the pinching\. This bounds the trace\-power terms of the MEAT objective\.

- 1.6 [`foundations/fact:pinch-id`](../claims/fact-pinch-id.tex): Pinched trace-power form of the conditional Rényi entropy ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/fact:pinch-id))

  **Non-normative summary.** For a purified input and a projective key measurement, the fixed\-marginal conditional Rényi entropy equals a trace\-power expression involving only the measured system's marginal\. This removes the explicit purifier from the computable branch objective\.

- 1.7 [`foundations/fact:concavity`](../claims/fact-concavity.tex): Concavity of the pinched trace-power functional ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/fact:concavity))

  **Non-normative summary.** The pinched trace\-power functional is concave on subnormalised states for every parameter in \[0, 1\]\. This is the analytic ingredient behind the convexity of the engine's objective\.


### 2 The round model

[Dependency view](group-02-the-round-model.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A run of n rounds is attacked by a sequence of channels, one per round, each delivering the per\-round system, with Alice's system too where the adversary supplies it, and carrying Eve's memory forward\. A list of operator constraints restricts each round's channel by adjoint conditions, which cut out an attack set, with a stronger condition for an entry scoped to one factor of a product system\. A register of any dimension, such as an adversary's memory, is approximated by compressing it onto a finite\-dimensional subspace, at a cost in trace norm that the state's weight outside the subspace bounds and that adds along a sequence of channels \([Lemma “Compressing a register onto a finite\-dimensional subspace”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:register-compression)\)\. The round model is stated once for a generic per\-round system, and the modules that analyse a protocol instantiate it at their own systems\. A family of objects describing a round is round\-homogeneous when the same objects describe every round and every round behaves as they describe whatever the other rounds do, so no device has memory across rounds; the modules' round assumptions assert it, or its sameness clause alone where they say so, of their own families\.

- 2.1 [`foundations/def:round-model`](../claims/def-round-model.tex): Round model, attack sequences and attack sets ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:round-model))

  **Non-normative summary.** Over n rounds of a per\-round system S, an attack sequence is one channel per round from Eve's register to S and her new memory, with Alice's system too when the adversary supplies it\. A list of operator constraints cuts out an attack set by adjoint conditions, and so the admissible attack sequences\.

- 2.2 [`foundations/def:round-homogeneity`](../claims/def-round-homogeneity.tex): Round homogeneity of a per-round family ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:round-homogeneity))

  **Non-normative summary.** A per\-round family is round\-homogeneous when one fixed list of objects describes every round \(sameness\) and every round behaves as they describe whatever the other rounds do \(no memory across rounds\)\. An assumption may assert sameness alone, and must then say so\.

- 2.3 [`foundations/lem:register-compression`](../claims/lem-register-compression.tex): Compressing a register onto a finite-dimensional subspace ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:register-compression))

  **Non-normative summary.** Compressing one register of a state onto a finite\-dimensional subspace moves the state by at most 2√δ \+ δ in trace norm, δ being its weight outside the subspace, whatever the other systems are\. The moves add along a sequence of channels, and every channel extends by an identity of any dimension\.


### 3 Binary64 numbers and exact sums

[Dependency view](group-03-binary64-numbers-and-exact-sums.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Declared values are binary64 numbers, read as the dyadic rationals they are\. A sum of them is accumulated left to right in a declared order, and it rounds nowhere when every binary64 addition is exact\. The main result, [Lemma “Exactness of a sum that rounds nowhere”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:exact-accumulation), shows that such a sum is the exact sum, so a predicate on it is a predicate on the declared values\. An example shows that the order of accumulation changes the computed sum\.

- 3.1 [`foundations/def:binary64`](../claims/def-binary64.tex): Binary64 numbers, declared values and dyadic lattices ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:binary64))

  **Non-normative summary.** A declared value is a binary64 number and denotes exactly the dyadic rational it is, with no exact object behind it\. A binary64 addition is exact precisely when the true sum is a binary64 number, and a lattice value at k is an integer multiple of two to the power −k of modulus at most one\.

- 3.2 [`foundations/lem:binary64-lattice`](../claims/lem-binary64-lattice.tex): Binary64 numbers on dyadic lattices ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:binary64-lattice))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every lattice value at an exponent k ≤ 53 is a binary64 number, and between two consecutive powers of two the binary64 numbers are the multiples of one power of two, in \[1/2, 1\) those of two to the power −53\. Representability of a declared entry is therefore a divisibility condition\.

- 3.3 [`foundations/def:declared-order`](../claims/def-declared-order.tex): Sums in a declared order and sums that round nowhere ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:declared-order))

  **Non-normative summary.** A sum of declared values is accumulated left to right in a declared order, and it rounds nowhere when every one of its binary64 additions is exact\. Binary64 addition is not associative, so the order is part of the datum\.

- 3.4 [`foundations/lem:exact-accumulation`](../claims/lem-exact-accumulation.tex): Exactness of a sum that rounds nowhere ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:exact-accumulation))

  **Non-normative summary.** A sequential sum rounds nowhere exactly when every exact partial sum is a binary64 number, and then the computed sum equals the exact sum at every step\. A predicate stated on such a sum is a predicate on the declared values themselves\.

- 3.5 [`foundations/rem:summation-order`](../claims/rem-summation-order.tex): Dependence of a computed sum on its order ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/rem:summation-order))

  **Non-normative summary.** Two orderings of the same three binary64 numbers have the same exact sum but different computed sums, and only one of them rounds nowhere\. A computed sum is therefore a determinate number only once its order is fixed\.


### 4 Declared data and exact admissibility

[Dependency view](group-04-declared-data-and-exact-admissibility.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Declared data are what an instance states and what a producer emits as its own declaration, and a consumer reads them across a module boundary\. An operator is a list of blocks against a sector map and is block diagonal by construction\. The section's main definition, [Definition “Exact admissibility of a declared operator family”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:exact-admissibility), states the five conditions every module's admission shares, and a counterexample shows why a completeness sum must round nowhere\.

- 4.1 [`foundations/def:declared-data`](../claims/def-declared-data.tex): Declared data and the parties to a module boundary ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:declared-data))

  **Non-normative summary.** Declared data are what an instance states, a supplier's output included, and what a producer emits as its own declaration\. A consumer reads declared data across a module boundary; a point a module computes itself is never declared\.

- 4.2 [`foundations/def:sector-map`](../claims/def-sector-map.tex): Sector maps and block-list operators ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:sector-map))

  **Non-normative summary.** A sector map names the blocks of an operator by a block kind and an ordered list of identifiers with dimensions\. An operator given as one array per block is block diagonal by construction, and its Hermiticity, positivity and completeness hold block by block\.

- 4.3 [`foundations/def:exact-admissibility`](../claims/def-exact-admissibility.tex): Exact admissibility of a declared operator family ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:exact-admissibility))

  **Non-normative summary.** A declared operator family is exactly admissible for a sector map with constant c when \(T\) its own sector map agrees, \(H\) every block is exactly Hermitian, \(P\) positive, \(C\) it sums to c times the identity and \(S\) its declared\-order sum rounds nowhere\. Each is a property of the declared numbers\.

- 4.4 [`foundations/rem:computed-completeness`](../claims/rem-computed-completeness.tex): A complete computed sum from an incomplete family ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/rem:computed-completeness))

  **Non-normative summary.** Three positive binary64 numbers can have computed sum exactly one while their true sum exceeds one\. A completeness test on the computed sum alone would admit them, which is why the rounds\-nowhere condition belongs to exact admissibility\.


### 5 The declared tolerance and the snap

[Dependency view](group-05-the-declared-tolerance-and-the-snap.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** An instance declares one tolerance, which bounds the defects it may repair in its own data, measured as entrywise defects of differences and as a floor on the least eigenvalue\. [Lemma “The positivity floor against entrywise closeness to the cone”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:floor-stricter) shows that the floor is the stricter of the two, by a factor of the dimension that is attained\. A snap replaces a declared family by an exactly admissible one on the declarer's lattice and records what it changed\. A measurement family has a reference, the model it is declared from, known exactly or through rational entrywise enclosures, and on some blocks also through an enclosed factor with an exact core \([Definition “Factor enclosure of a block”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:factor-enclosure)\)\. Entries known to be zero fix the structural support of each element, which no snap may enlarge, save the flagged last resort of [Requirement “Support\-safe snaps of a measurement family”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:support-safe-snap) \(iv\); a block is kept where the snap is decided equal to the data it is made from, and moved otherwise\. An enclosure entry whose disc contains zero may be recentred at zero and widened, and the reference is unchanged \([Lemma “Zeroing the centres an enclosure cannot tell from zero”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:recentred-enclosure)\)\. A declarer may hand the reference beside the snap, and a block whose snapped data nothing the key length rests on reads, save the points and the acceptance data, is evaluated off its snap \([Definition “A reference handed beside a snap; a block evaluated off its snap”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:evaluated-off-snap)\)\.

- 5.1 [`foundations/def:tolerance`](../claims/def-tolerance.tex): The declared tolerance and the defects it bounds ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:tolerance))

  **Non-normative summary.** The declared tolerance is a binary64 number in \[0, 1\) that bounds the defects of an instance's own data before they are snapped\. A defect of a difference is an entrywise maximum modulus, and positivity is measured by a floor on the least eigenvalue\.

- 5.2 [`foundations/lem:floor-stricter`](../claims/lem-floor-stricter.tex): The positivity floor against entrywise closeness to the cone ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:floor-stricter))

  **Non-normative summary.** A Hermitian matrix with least eigenvalue at least −δ lies within entrywise distance δ of the positive cone, but entrywise distance δ only guarantees a least eigenvalue of −dδ in dimension d, and that factor is attained\. The two tests are different quantities and never interchangeable\.

- 5.3 [`foundations/def:exact-reference`](../claims/def-exact-reference.tex): Reference of a measurement family, exact or enclosed ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:exact-reference))

  **Non-normative summary.** A reference is the model a measurement family is declared from: Hermitian, positive and complete blocks, each read through an entrywise enclosure, a Gaussian\-rational centre with rational entrywise radii\. It is exact when every radius is zero, and every decision about it is then on rationals\.

- 5.4 [`foundations/def:factor-enclosure`](../claims/def-factor-enclosure.tex): Factor enclosure of a block ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:factor-enclosure))

  **Non-normative summary.** A factor enclosure knows a matrix as L W L\*, with L\* the adjoint of some L in a box of complex matrices around Gaussian\-rational centres and W an exact positive diagonal core; its members are those products\. A reference may carry one on a block beside the block's entrywise enclosure\.

- 5.5 [`foundations/def:snap`](../claims/def-snap.tex): Snapping declared data onto a lattice ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:snap))

  **Non-normative summary.** A snap replaces data by lattice data of the same shape that meet their conditions exactly, entries known to be zero fixing each element's structural support\. A block of a measurement snap is kept where it is decided equal to the data it is made from, and moved otherwise\.

- 5.6 [`foundations/lem:recentred-enclosure`](../claims/lem-recentred-enclosure.tex): Zeroing the centres an enclosure cannot tell from zero ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:recentred-enclosure))

  **Non-normative summary.** Zeroing every centre entry whose enclosure contains zero, and adding its modulus bound to the radius, gives another entrywise enclosure of the same matrix; a reference so recentred is the same reference\. Any enclosure of a Hermitian matrix becomes an entrywise one by averaging\.

- 5.7 [`foundations/def:evaluated-off-snap`](../claims/def-evaluated-off-snap.tex): A reference handed beside a snap; a block evaluated off its snap ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:evaluated-off-snap))

  **Non-normative summary.** A declarer hands a reference beside a snap when it passes on the reference's centres and radii with it, unaltered\. A block is evaluated off its snap when nothing its key length is computed from reads the snapped block, save the points and the values fixed before the first round\.


### 6 Domination and its limits

[Dependency view](group-06-domination-and-its-limits.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A family G dominates a family F at a block weight q when G − \(1−q\)F is positive semidefinite for every outcome; G is then a mixture of F and a complete residual family, which is what lets a noise channel reduce G to F plus flags of weight q\. A dominated family adds no support, though it may lose some, and domination composes and survives trusted classical post\-processing \([Lemma “Elementary properties of domination”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:domination)\); for a family that absorbs its residue in one element only that element constrains the weight \([Lemma “Domination of a family that absorbs its residue in one element”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:absorbing-domination)\)\. A reference can dominate a lattice family at a weight below one only along rationally spanned directions \([Lemma “Rationality obstruction to the domination of a lattice family”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:rational-obstruction)\), and an exact decision on a rational enclosure gives a sufficient condition for the positivity of matrices with irrational entries \([Lemma “Positive semidefiniteness from an entrywise enclosure”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:enclosure-psd)\)\. No requirement relates a snap to its model by domination; the section is kept for a declared device weight\. The closing remark explains what a weight does not control, why an idealised model with an element whose support has no rational direction needs a noise floor before it dominates a lattice family at a small weight, and that reductions other than domination are not bound by that obstruction\.

- 6.1 [`foundations/def:domination`](../claims/def-domination.tex): Domination of one operator family by another at block weights ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:domination))

  **Non-normative summary.** A family G dominates a family F at block weights q when G − \(1−q\)F is positive semidefinite for every outcome on every block, one weight per block\. The residual family is what G holds beyond the weighted F, rescaled by the weight\.

- 6.2 [`foundations/lem:domination`](../claims/lem-domination.tex): Elementary properties of domination ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:domination))

  **Non-normative summary.** Domination is upward closed in the weight, holds below one exactly when each support of F lies in that of G, is equality at weight zero, composes multiplicatively and survives classical post\-processing\. G is then a mixture of F and a complete residual family\.

- 6.3 [`foundations/lem:absorbing-domination`](../claims/lem-absorbing-domination.tex): Domination of a family that absorbs its residue in one element ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:absorbing-domination))

  **Non-normative summary.** When every element but one is rounded down below the reference element and the remaining one takes the rest, only that designated element constrains the weight\. A weight below one exists exactly when the absorbed residue lies in its support, and it is at most residue norm over least eigenvalue\.

- 6.4 [`foundations/lem:rational-obstruction`](../claims/lem-rational-obstruction.tex): Rationality obstruction to the domination of a lattice family ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:rational-obstruction))

  **Non-normative summary.** A family with Gaussian\-rational entries that G dominates at a weight below one has every support inside the largest rational subspace of the matching support of G\. With completeness the weight is at least the largest value of G on the complement of that subspace, over the constant\.

- 6.5 [`foundations/lem:enclosure-psd`](../claims/lem-enclosure-psd.tex): Positive semidefiniteness from an entrywise enclosure ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:enclosure-psd))

  **Non-normative summary.** A Hermitian matrix is at least μ times the identity when a Gaussian\-rational centre within entrywise radii r is at least ρ \+ μ times it, ρ the largest row sum of r\. Every hypothesis is a comparison of rationals, so matrices with irrational entries are decided in exact arithmetic\.

- 6.6 [`foundations/rem:noise-floor`](../claims/rem-noise-floor.tex): What domination controls, and why it needs a noise floor ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/rem:noise-floor))

  **Non-normative summary.** A weight at which a model dominates a lattice family shows that the family adds no support, not how far it lowers an element\. An element with no rational direction in its support forces a weight of at least its top eigenvalue over the constant; noise in the model removes that bound\.


### 7 The exact island, enclosures and the tiers

[Dependency view](group-07-the-exact-island-enclosures-and-the-tiers.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The exact island is where the engine computes on exact rationals, namely every decision that data satisfy the conditions their declaration states, a snap's decisions on its output and the decisions on a reference among them, a producer's construction of its emission, the exact establishment of the acceptance\-side iterate and of the acceptance\-side minimum over the acceptance set as its consumer reads it, and the certification of a lower bound on the feasible\-side minimum\. A least eigenvalue is bounded from below exactly from any approximate eigenbasis, by a Gershgorin bound corrected for the basis' defect from orthogonality \([Lemma “Least\-eigenvalue lower bound from an approximate eigenbasis”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:least-eigenvalue-bound)\)\. The bound moves across a ball around an exact matrix, and an entrywise enclosure fixes the rank of every matrix it contains once its exactly zero rows are dropped and the rest is shown positive definite \([Corollary “Least eigenvalue and rank over an enclosure”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/cor:least-eigenvalue-over-enclosure)\), a factor enclosure fixes the rank and support of each of its members once the Gram matrix of its columns is shown positive definite throughout \([Corollary “Rank over a factor enclosure”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/cor:rank-over-factor-enclosure)\), and powers of positive definite matrices and their divided\-difference maps move by explicit constants under a perturbation \([Lemma “Perturbation bounds for matrix functions”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:matrix-function-perturbation)\)\. A reported key rate carries one of three tiers, estimate, first\-order or certified, and no other\.

- 7.1 [`foundations/def:exact-island`](../claims/def-exact-island.tex): The exact island ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:exact-island))

  **Non-normative summary.** The exact island holds every decision that data meet their declared conditions, those on a reference among them, a producer's construction of its emission, and certificates of the optimiser's two linear minima\. The tolerance ceiling, the rest of the optimiser and binary64 proposals are outside it\.

- 7.2 [`foundations/lem:least-eigenvalue-bound`](../claims/lem-least-eigenvalue-bound.tex): Least-eigenvalue lower bound from an approximate eigenbasis ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:least-eigenvalue-bound))

  **Non-normative summary.** For a Hermitian matrix and any real V whose Gram matrix lies within θ \< 1 of the identity in Frobenius norm, the Gershgorin bound γ of the real embedding conjugated by V gives λ\_min ≥ γ/\(1\+θ\), or γ/\(1−θ\) if γ \< 0\. On rational data every step is exact, and V decides tightness only\.

- 7.3 [`foundations/cor:least-eigenvalue-over-enclosure`](../claims/cor-least-eigenvalue-over-enclosure.tex): Least eigenvalue and rank over an enclosure ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/cor:least-eigenvalue-over-enclosure))

  **Non-normative summary.** A Hermitian matrix within ρ of a Hermitian M in Frobenius norm has its spectrum within ρ of M's extreme eigenvalues\. An entrywise enclosure whose exactly zero rows are dropped and whose rest certifies a positive least eigenvalue fixes the rank and support of every Hermitian matrix it contains\.

- 7.4 [`foundations/cor:rank-over-factor-enclosure`](../claims/cor-rank-over-factor-enclosure.tex): Rank over a factor enclosure ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/cor:rank-over-factor-enclosure))

  **Non-normative summary.** A factor enclosure with no column holds only the zero matrix\. One whose Gram matrix L\*L has a positive floor over its box fixes, at every member, the rank, its number of columns, and the support, the span of that member's columns\.

- 7.5 [`foundations/lem:matrix-function-perturbation`](../claims/lem-matrix-function-perturbation.tex): Perturbation bounds for matrix functions ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/lem:matrix-function-perturbation))

  **Non-normative summary.** On Hermitian matrices with spectra in an interval, a function with derivative bounded by L moves at most L times the perturbation in Frobenius norm, and the divided\-difference map of x^p at most p\(1−p\)a^\(p−2\) times it above a floor a\. Close arguments get a second\-order bound\.

- 7.6 [`foundations/def:tiers`](../claims/def-tiers.tex): The tiers of a reported key rate ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/def:tiers))

  **Non-normative summary.** A reported key rate carries one of three tiers: estimate, a returned point's objective, claiming no bound; first\-order, a lower bound on the minimum under named hypotheses; or certified, such a bound with every step from the data exact or rigorously enclosed, no hypothesis about arithmetic\.


### 8 The snapped-measurement assumption

[Dependency view](group-08-the-snapped-measurement-assumption.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A moved block of a measurement snap is related to its model by no claim\. Where the run reads it, because no reference reaches the evaluation, or the evaluation on the reference does not count, or the key length is formed from a number the run certified on the snapped family in its place, the analysis of the run is an analysis of the snapped family, the more so where a flagged last resort gave an element support the data deny\. The one assumption of the module, [Assumption “The device measures the snapped family where the run reads the snap”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/ass:snapped-measurement), is the premise under which that analysis describes the device: on the blocks the record names, the device measures the snapped family as recorded, in place of the model there, and for a producer's snap of its own emission, what the run concludes from the emission holds of the snapped family\. A block evaluated off its snap is never among them\. A run with no such block, or a run that declares the strict option, does not rest on it\.

- 8.1 [`foundations/ass:snapped-measurement`](../claims/ass-snapped-measurement.tex): The device measures the snapped family where the run reads the snap ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/ass:snapped-measurement))

  **Non-normative summary.** On every moved block of a snap that the run does not evaluate off its snap, the device measures the snapped family; for a producer's snap of its own emission, what the run concludes from the emission holds of the snapped family there\.


## Part II: Engine requirements

**Non-normative summary.** What an implementation of this module must do, record or refuse; each requirement names the result that motivates it\.

### 9 Admission

[Dependency view](group-09-admission.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The section's main requirement, [Requirement “Exact admissibility at every module boundary”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:boundary-admission), is that declared operator data are exactly admissible at every module boundary\. Every consumer decides its admission predicates exactly and refuses otherwise, and it alters nothing it reads except by rounding an emitted bound in a direction its producer licenses as weakening\. A sum is decided in the order declared with its data\.

- 9.1 [`foundations/req:boundary-admission`](../claims/req-boundary-admission.tex): Exact admissibility at every module boundary ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:boundary-admission))

  **Non-normative summary.** Declared operator data cross every module boundary exactly admissible, so every sum a predicate reads is the exact sum\. The declarer makes them so, or, for data an instance passes on unchanged, the consumer's exact decision on entry does, and a consumer that finds otherwise refuses them\.

- 9.2 [`foundations/req:consumer-decides-exactly`](../claims/req-consumer-decides-exactly.tex): Exact decision by every consumer ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:consumer-decides-exactly))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every consumer decides each admission predicate on declared data exactly, has no tolerance input, and refuses with a reason naming the failed predicate\. It repairs nothing it was handed and alters nothing, except that it may round an emitted bound in a direction its producer licenses as weakening\.

- 9.3 [`foundations/req:declared-order`](../claims/req-declared-order.tex): Summation order declared with the data ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:declared-order))

  **Non-normative summary.** Where a predicate on declared data is a sum, its order of accumulation is declared with the alphabet that indexes it and travels with the data\. Every consumer, and every declarer, that decides the predicate decides it in that order\.


### 10 The tolerance, norms and snapping

[Dependency view](group-10-the-tolerance-norms-and-snapping.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** One declared tolerance per instance caps the defect an instance may repair in its own declaration, and it governs nothing else; a probability vector prepared from ordinary numerical inputs is admitted by the input preparation's own rule\. A bound in one norm is never read as a bound in another, and only the declarer of data snaps them, on record\. A block of a snap of a measurement family known exactly on the lattice is kept, and every other block is moved by one procedure that aims at fidelity; a moved block that the run does not evaluate off its snap is disclosed with its cause, one of four groups and a fix, or refused where the run declares the strict option \([Requirement “Snaps of a measurement family: kept, or moved and disclosed”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:snap-reference)\)\. No snap revives an element, row or column that the data state to be zero, save a flagged last resort where nothing else restores the conditions of a snap \([Requirement “Support\-safe snaps of a measurement family”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:support-safe-snap)\)\.

- 10.1 [`foundations/req:declared-tolerance`](../claims/req-declared-tolerance.tex): One declared tolerance per instance ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:declared-tolerance))

  **Non-normative summary.** One tolerance in \[0, 1\) per instance, with no default, caps the defect, measured in binary64, that an instance may repair in what it declares\. A family snapped on an exact reference has no defect, supplier output is decided by its consumer, a prepared probability vector by its own rule\.

- 10.2 [`foundations/req:tolerance-scope`](../claims/req-tolerance-scope.tex): Scope of the declared tolerance ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:tolerance-scope))

  **Non-normative summary.** The declared tolerance governs only the repair of an instance's own data\. Sets defined by declared data stay exact, a window on a computed object is another declared number, and a physical bound or a slack on a supplier's evaluation is never derived from the tolerance\.

- 10.3 [`foundations/req:norm-conversion`](../claims/req-norm-conversion.tex): Explicit conversion between norms ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:norm-conversion))

  **Non-normative summary.** A bound stated in one norm is never read as a bound in another\. A declared number derived from a bound in another norm carries the conversion factor between the two norms, written down and recorded by the party that declares it\.

- 10.4 [`foundations/req:who-snaps`](../claims/req-who-snaps.tex): Snapping by the declarer only ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:who-snaps))

  **Non-normative summary.** Only the declarer of data snaps them, once and before any consumer reads them: an instance its own declaration, a producer its own emission\. No party alters data it was handed, save a consumer rounding an emitted bound in a direction its producer licenses as weakening, and every snap is recorded\.

- 10.5 [`foundations/req:snap-reference`](../claims/req-snap-reference.tex): Snaps of a measurement family: kept, or moved and disclosed ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:snap-reference))

  **Non-normative summary.** A measurement snap keeps the blocks known exactly on the lattice and moves the others by one fidelity\-first, support\-safe procedure\. A moved block not evaluated off its snap rests on the snap premise, recorded with cause, group and fix; the strict option refuses it\.

- 10.6 [`foundations/req:support-safe-snap`](../claims/req-support-safe-snap.tex): Support-safe snaps of a measurement family ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:support-safe-snap))

  **Non-normative summary.** No snap of a measurement family revives an element, row or column that the data state to be zero: a positivity lift and a completeness residue go only where the receiving element's structural support holds them\. Where that fails, a flagged uniform lift is the last resort\.


### 11 Emission

[Dependency view](group-11-emission.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** These are the three emission rules\. The lattice condition binds data declared at a lattice exponent, emitted bounds are rounded in the direction that weakens them, and a producer establishes exact admissibility of what it emits before emitting it \([Requirement “Exact verification before emission”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:exact-emission)\), or refuses\.

- 11.1 [`foundations/req:declared-lattice`](../claims/req-declared-lattice.tex): The declared lattice condition ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:declared-lattice))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every entry of operator data declared at a lattice exponent k is a lattice value at k, and so a binary64 number\. The condition binds data an instance snaps or declares to a consumer that admits only at its lattice, and the emission of a producer that declares one, and no other data\.

- 11.2 [`foundations/req:rounding-direction`](../claims/req-rounding-direction.tex): Rounding direction of emitted bounds ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:rounding-direction))

  **Non-normative summary.** An emitted scalar bound is rounded onto the lattice in the direction that weakens the claim it carries, never to nearest, and the direction is recorded\. A complementary quantity is recomputed from rounded weights and never rounded on its own\.

- 11.3 [`foundations/req:exact-emission`](../claims/req-exact-emission.tex): Exact verification before emission ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:exact-emission))

  **Non-normative summary.** A producer emits a measurement family only after establishing exactly, on the numbers it will emit, the sector map, Hermiticity, positivity, completeness, a declared\-order sum that rounds nowhere and, where it declares a lattice, the lattice condition\. Otherwise it refuses or emits a disclosed snap\.


### 12 Arithmetic and tiers

[Dependency view](group-12-arithmetic-and-tiers.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Every decision, certification and construction of the exact island is computed exactly, a binary64 candidate entering only through one of them, and nothing outside the island carries an error budget, save a slack a supplier spends on its own floating\-point evaluation under a named assumption; a rigorous enclosure, whose operations bound their own rounding, assumes none\. Every run reports each tier of its key rate it reaches and names the strongest, which carries its claim\.

- 12.1 [`foundations/req:exact-island`](../claims/req-exact-island.tex): Exact arithmetic on the island, unbudgeted rounding elsewhere ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:exact-island))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every decision, certification and construction of the exact island is computed exactly, a binary64 candidate entering only through one of them\. Outside it no result rests on a floating\-point error budget, save a supplier's slack under a named assumption; rigorously enclosed steps assume none\.

- 12.2 [`foundations/req:tiers`](../claims/req-tiers.tex): Reporting of the tiers ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-foundations/req:tiers))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every run that reports a key rate reports it at each tier it reaches and names the strongest, whose number is the headline and gives the key length\. No module labels a number with a tier its checks do not support, and a request for an unknown tier is refused\.
