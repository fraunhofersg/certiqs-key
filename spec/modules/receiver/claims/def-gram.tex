The admissibility defined at the end is an exact condition, while a declared
family is admitted by a floating-point test, in the \tref{evaluation model} of
Definition~\ref{def:evaluation-model}.
Theorem~\ref{thm:exact-and-evaluated} states what this contract asserts about
the exact family and what about its floating-point evaluation.

\begin{definition}\label{def:gram}
The \term[The positive operators on the declared input modes, one per detector port, whose quadratic form is the squared norm of the amplitude a receiver instrument sends to that port.]{port Gram matrices} of $\Recv$ are the operators on
$\mathbb{C}^{\Inp}$
\begin{equation}\label{eq:gramdef}
  A_k\coloneqq\Gmat_{\Inp}^\dagger\,\Emd{\{k\}}\,\Gmat_{\Inp},
  \qquad k=1,\dots,K ,
\end{equation}
and for $J\subseteq[K]$ we write $A_J\coloneqq\sum_{k\in J}A_k$ and
$A_\Sigma\coloneqq A_{[K]}$. The \term[The identity on the declared input modes minus the sum of all port Grams.]{loss deficiency} is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:deficiency}
  \defic\coloneqq\id_{\Inp}-A_\Sigma .
\end{equation}
A family $\{A_k\}_{k=1}^{K}$ of operators on a finite-dimensional space is an
\term[A finite family of positive semidefinite operators whose sum is at most the identity; an exact condition, distinct from exact admissibility of declared data.]{admissible Gram family} when $A_k\succeq0$ for every $k$ and
$\sum_kA_k\preceq\id$. This is an exact condition; on a \emph{declared} family
it is \emph{decided} in floating point at the
\tref[receiver tolerances]{receiver tolerance}
$\varepsilon_2$, by Definition~\ref{def:tolmap}\,(i).
\end{definition}
