## Module summary

Summaries explain purpose and content. They are non-normative; the claim statements, proofs and declared assumptions remain authoritative.

**Non-normative summary.** This contract turns a declared passive optical receiver into the click\-pattern measurement it performs and into lower bounds on its multiclick weight on every photon\-number block above a cutoff N; both are recorded in the receiver's artifact, which the flag\-state squasher of the reductions module consumes\. A receiver may be declared by its port Grams \(route \(i\)\), by an instrument matrix \(route \(ii\)\) or by a graph of catalogue components \(route \(iii\)\); the three routes converge on the port Gram family, which determines the click statistics exactly\. A receiver fed by its own probe source as well as by the channel needs a submodule that constructs its setting family and is otherwise refused\. The main results of this core module are the click\-pattern POVM, which follows by inclusion and exclusion and is block diagonal in the total input photon number \([Theorem “Click\-pattern POVM”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:click-povm)\); its declared coarse\-graining into the protocol's alphabet, under which lower bounds on disjoint raw outcomes add \([Theorem “Coarse\-graining of the click\-pattern POVM”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:coarse-graining)\); the weight in \[0, 1\] emitted for each probe setting, which bounds that setting's multiclick element from below on every block of channel photon number above N \([Theorem “Multiclick bounds carried by the emitted weights”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:emitted-bound)\); and the range of the weights the artifact carries in its strengthening slot, one per outcome set and setting, which are non\-negative and sum to at most one at every setting \([Proposition “Range of the strengthening\-slot weights”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/prop:slotvalid)\)\. The weight comes from an analytic lower bound on the smallest eigenvalue of every multiclick block, made uniform over all blocks above N either by a collapse lemma, which reduces the non\-negative part of the infimum over those blocks to that of the value at the first of them, or by the minimum over a finite window of blocks capped by an analytic term for the tail beyond it; an optional exact window uses lower bounds on the smallest eigenvalues of finitely many blocks\. Every statement is exact unless it names a receiver tolerance or the eigenvalue slack: where the declaration defines an admissible receiver exactly, the weights are evaluated rigorously, in exact rational arithmetic from enclosures of the model taken on the conservative side, and are lower bounds by theorem with no premise about arithmetic \([Theorem “The weight on enclosed bound data bounds the multiclick element”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:enclosed-weight)\), while elsewhere they are evaluated in binary64 floating point, residuals diagnose the evaluation, and an exact window rests on the assumption that each computed eigenvalue less a declared slack does not exceed the exact one\. Separately, for every declaration that defines an admissible receiver exactly, whatever its parameters, the photon\-number blocks are enclosed rigorously in exact rational arithmetic, from enclosures of the square roots, cosines and sines the construction takes \([Theorem “Rigorous enclosures of the photon\-number blocks”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:enclosed-blocks)\); where the port Grams are Gaussian\-rational the blocks of at most one photon are known exactly \([Theorem “Exact entries of the blocks on the exact route”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:exact-blocks)\), and, read through a port dilation, a block of a receiver without probe inputs can be given besides in enclosed factor form, which fixes its rank where the Gram matrix of its columns has a positive floor, as no entrywise box with positive radii can for a block of lower rank whose support no coordinate vectors span \([Theorem “Photon\-number blocks in enclosed factor form”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:factor-blocks)\)\. These enclosures are the reference a snap of the emitted blocks is certified against\. The results are conditional on the device assumptions of the Assumptions section, among them a trusted, exactly characterised, passive device whose declared modes carry every path to a detector: the main results rest only on the few of these their proofs use, and the others enter the weight through the corollaries that apply [Theorem “Multiclick bounds carried by the emitted weights”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:emitted-bound) to each setting family\. Detector imperfections and the component catalogue are submodules, each with main results of its own\.

## Part I: Mathematics

### 1 Modes, Fock space and the mode algebra

[Dependency view](group-01-modes-fock-space-and-the-mode-algebra.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A receiver acts on a finite set of declared optical modes, and its photon statistics live in the Fock space over them\. This section lifts a matrix on the modes to every photon\-number sector and records the algebra the later proofs run on: embedding, products and adjoints preserve contractions and elements on disjoint modes commute \([Proposition “Algebra of embedded local elements”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/prop:mode-algebra)\), and the lift respects composition, adjoints and projectors \([Lemma “Functoriality of the Fock lift”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:fock-functor)\), also for maps between two mode spaces, where the lift of a direct sum is a tensor product \([Lemma “The lift of maps between mode spaces and of direct sums”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:fock-maps)\)\. The lift of 1 − A is called a lifted no\-click operator because, with A the sum of the port Grams of a set of ports, it is the operator for the event that no port of the set clicks, pulled back to the declared inputs \([Theorem “Port Grams and dilation independence”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:port-grams)\)\. Its spectrum on each photon\-number block is computed from the eigenvalues of A \([Lemma “Spectrum of a lifted no\-click operator on a photon\-number block”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:fock-block-spectrum)\), which is where the analytic high\-photon bound starts\. The normal\-ordered form is stated for comparison with the optics literature; no proof uses it\.

- 1.1 [`receiver/def:modes`](../claims/def-modes.tex): Declared mode space ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:modes))

  **Non-normative summary.** A declared mode space is a finite, totally ordered set of optical modes, each labelled by a path, a polarization and a reserved temporal or spectral index that takes one declared value\. Matrices on the modes follow this order; on the input modes the channel modes come first \([Definition “Receiver instrument”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:instrument)\)\.

- 1.2 [`receiver/def:fock`](../claims/def-fock.tex): Fock space and the second-quantization functor ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:fock))

  **Non-normative summary.** The Fock space over a finite\-dimensional space is the direct sum of its symmetric photon\-number sectors, and a linear map lifts to it sector by sector\. Every photon\-number block of the receiver's measurement is assembled from blocks of such lifts\.

- 1.3 [`receiver/def:embed`](../claims/def-embed.tex): Local element and its embedding ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:embed))

  **Non-normative summary.** A local element is a contraction on a subset of the modes, its support, and its global embedding acts as the identity on every other mode\. Embedding is what lets component descriptions compose as matrices on the whole mode space\.

- 1.4 [`receiver/prop:mode-algebra`](../claims/prop-mode-algebra.tex): Algebra of embedded local elements ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/prop:mode-algebra))

  **Non-normative summary.** Embedding preserves contractivity, products of contractions are contractions, elements on disjoint supports commute, and embedding respects adjoints and unitarity\. Graph compilation rests on these four facts\.

- 1.5 [`receiver/rem:overlap`](../claims/rem-overlap.tex): Disjoint mode supports as the commutation hypothesis ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:overlap))

  **Non-normative summary.** The contract's only commutation result, [Proposition “Algebra of embedded local elements”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/prop:mode-algebra) \(iii\), needs disjoint mode supports; being unrelated in a declared graph is not enough\. Unrelated elements that share a mode can depend on their order, which is why a well\-formed graph must order them\.

- 1.6 [`receiver/lem:fock-functor`](../claims/lem-fock-functor.tex): Functoriality of the Fock lift ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:fock-functor))

  **Non-normative summary.** The Fock lift respects composition and adjoints, preserves every photon\-number sector, and takes unitaries, contractions and projectors to unitaries, contractions and projectors\. The lift of a unitary implements the mode transformation on the annihilation operators\.

- 1.7 [`receiver/lem:fock-maps`](../claims/lem-fock-maps.tex): The lift of maps between mode spaces and of direct sums ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:fock-maps))

  **Non-normative summary.** The Fock lift respects products and adjoints of maps between two mode spaces and takes contractions to contractions and isometries to isometries\. On disjoint mode sets the occupation identification is unitary, and the lift of a direct sum is the tensor product of the lifts\.

- 1.8 [`receiver/rem:migdal`](../claims/rem-migdal.tex): Mode and particle descriptions in the literature ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:migdal))

  **Non-normative summary.** The lift of a unitary acts on n photons as its n\-fold tensor power on the symmetric subspace, the standard dictionary between the mode and particle descriptions\. A published statement of it corroborates the lemma; no claim depends on the citation\.

- 1.9 [`receiver/lem:fock-block-spectrum`](../claims/lem-fock-block-spectrum.tex): Spectrum of a lifted no-click operator on a photon-number block ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:fock-block-spectrum))

  **Non-normative summary.** On the n\-photon block, the lift of 1 − A has as eigenvalues the products of n factors 1 − λ over the eigenvalues λ of A, so it lies between the n\-th powers of 1 − λmax and 1 − λmin, both attained\. The analytic high\-photon bound of the receiver starts from these two extremes\.

- 1.10 [`receiver/lem:fock-monotone`](../claims/lem-fock-monotone.tex): Order monotonicity of the Fock lift ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:fock-monotone))

  **Non-normative summary.** The Fock lift preserves the Loewner order on positive semidefinite operators\. A larger detection operator A therefore gives a smaller lifted no\-click operator, the lift of 1 − A\.

- 1.11 [`receiver/prop:normord`](../claims/prop-normord.tex): Normal-ordered form of a lifted no-click operator ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/prop:normord))

  **Non-normative summary.** The lift of 1 − A equals the normally ordered exponential of minus the quadratic form of A in the mode operators, a series that is a finite sum on each photon\-number block\. It is the form of the optics literature; no proof of this contract uses it\.

- 1.12 [`receiver/rem:konig`](../claims/rem-konig.tex): The single-mode normal-ordering identity in the literature ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:konig))

  **Non-normative summary.** The single\-mode step of the normal\-ordered form is the published identity that the normally ordered exponential of z times the number operator is \(1 \+ z\) to the number operator, and its λ = 1 case is the perfect on/off detector\. Both citations corroborate; neither is used\.

- 1.13 [`receiver/rem:whyprojector`](../claims/rem-whyprojector.tex): Derivations without normal ordering ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:whyprojector))

  **Non-normative summary.** The normal\-ordered form is stated for comparison and used in no proof\. Every operator identity from the port Grams to the emitted weight follows from the functoriality of the Fock lift and vacuum elimination, block by block in finite dimension, with no convergence question\.


### 2 The receiver instrument and its physical model

[Dependency view](group-02-the-receiver-instrument-and-its-physical-model.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A receiver instrument is a declared mode space, a split of the declared inputs into channel modes, through which the adversary's signal enters, and probe modes, disjoint detector ports and a contractive instrument matrix; its port Grams are the compact data the measurement is derived from\. The probe modes carry the receiver's own probe light, in a declared state for each probe setting, which the receiver chooses privately in each round\. A contraction becomes a physical device by a unitary dilation with vacuum environment modes, and the detectors are commuting threshold measurements on the ports\. Vacuum on every undeclared input makes the restriction to the declared input columns exact \([Lemma “Exactness of vacuum elimination”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:vacelim)\); a probe mode carries its declared state and is never eliminated\.

- 2.1 [`receiver/def:instrument`](../claims/def-instrument.tex): Receiver instrument ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:instrument))

  **Non-normative summary.** A receiver instrument is a declared mode space with its inputs split into at least one channel mode and the probe modes, disjoint detector ports and a contractive instrument matrix\. Undeclared inputs carry vacuum, and a mode in no port is undetected\.

- 2.2 [`receiver/def:gram`](../claims/def-gram.tex): Port Grams and the loss deficiency ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:gram))

  **Non-normative summary.** The port Gram matrix of port k is the positive operator on the declared inputs whose quadratic form is the squared norm of the amplitude reaching that port, and the loss deficiency is the identity minus their sum\. These Grams are the compact data the click measurement is derived from\.

- 2.3 [`receiver/def:probe`](../claims/def-probe.tex): Probe declaration ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:probe))

  **Non-normative summary.** A probe declaration is a finite alphabet of probe settings, vacuum among them, with a declared probe state per setting that is diagonal in the occupation basis\. In the Gaussian tier each state is a product of thermal states given by their mean photon numbers\.

- 2.4 [`receiver/lem:dilation`](../claims/lem-dilation.tex): Unitary dilation of a contraction ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:dilation))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every contractive instrument matrix is the input\-to\-output block of a unitary on the modes together with a copy of them as environment modes\. The amplitude the matrix removes leaves through those extra outputs, which makes the matrix a passive physical device\.

- 2.5 [`receiver/def:physmodel`](../claims/def-physmodel.tex): The physical measurement model ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:physmodel))

  **Non-normative summary.** The receiver's measurement is a unitary dilation of its instrument matrix applied to the declared inputs with vacuum everywhere else, followed by commuting threshold measurements on the detector ports\. The input\-side click POVM is derived from this model\.

- 2.6 [`receiver/lem:vacelim`](../claims/lem-vacelim.tex): Exactness of vacuum elimination ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:vacelim))

  **Non-normative summary.** Compressing the lift of any operator by vacuum on the modes outside a coordinate subspace gives exactly the lift of its block on that subspace, and the measurement isometry is an isometry\. Dropping the undeclared input columns is therefore exact under the vacuum assumption\.

- 2.7 [`receiver/rem:lossvsprobe`](../claims/rem-lossvsprobe.tex): Loss ancillas and probe inputs ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:lossvsprobe))

  **Non-normative summary.** Vacuum elimination removes an input mode only because it carries vacuum; a declared source mode carries the probe state and stays a column of the instrument matrix\. Eliminating it would replace the probe by vacuum and describe a different device, a security\-relevant mismatch\.


### 3 Component graphs and compilation

[Dependency view](group-03-component-graphs-and-compilation.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A receiver may be declared as a directed graph of catalogue components\. The graph determines an instrument matrix when it is well formed \(parameters in their domains, acyclic, and any two elements that share a mode ordered\): every topological order then compiles to the same contraction \([Theorem “Well\-definedness of compilation”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:compilation-well-defined)\)\. A well\-formed graph, with its declared input modes, ports and efficiencies, then gives a receiver instrument \([Corollary “Receiver instrument from a well\-formed graph declaration”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/cor:compiled-instrument)\)\. Without the ordering condition two sorts of the same graph can give different devices, which is why it is a well\-formedness condition rather than a modelling remark\.

- 3.1 [`receiver/def:catalogue`](../claims/def-catalogue.tex): Component catalogue ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:catalogue))

  **Non-normative summary.** A component catalogue is a finite set of entry types, each fixing a support shape, a parameter domain and a rule that yields a local element\. The catalogue submodule declares the entries and proves that each maps its whole domain into local elements\.

- 3.2 [`receiver/def:graph`](../claims/def-graph.tex): Declared element graph ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:graph))

  **Non-normative summary.** A declared element graph is well formed when every element's parameters lie in their domain, the graph is acyclic, and any two elements sharing a mode are ordered by it\. Support comparability keeps an arbitrary sort from supplying a physical ordering the declaration left open\.

- 3.3 [`receiver/def:compile`](../claims/def-compile.tex): Compilation of a declared element graph ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:compile))

  **Non-normative summary.** The compiled instrument matrix is the product of the embedded elements along a linear extension, later elements multiplying from the left, followed by the terminal detector\-efficiency layer\. This fixes the matrix convention of the whole receiver contract\.

- 3.4 [`receiver/thm:compilation-well-defined`](../claims/thm-compilation-well-defined.tex): Well-definedness of compilation ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:compilation-well-defined))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every linear extension of a well\-formed graph compiles to the same contraction before the efficiency layer, which is unitary exactly when every element is lossless, and well\-formedness is decidable by finite inspection\. A well\-formed route\-\(iii\) graph therefore determines its instrument matrix\.

- 3.5 [`receiver/cor:compiled-instrument`](../claims/cor-compiled-instrument.tex): Receiver instrument from a well-formed graph declaration ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/cor:compiled-instrument))

  **Non-normative summary.** A well\-formed graph with a declared mode space, input split, port map and efficiencies yields a receiver instrument whose matrix does not depend on the sort\. This joins the graph route to the object the measurement theorems are stated of\.

- 3.6 [`receiver/rem:e3`](../claims/rem-e3.tex): Order dependence without support comparability ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:e3))

  **Non-normative summary.** An acyclic graph of a rotation and a polarizer on the same two modes, with no edge between them, compiles at generic angles to two different contractions under its two sorts\. Acyclicity alone therefore does not make a declared graph determine an instrument\.

- 3.7 [`receiver/rem:edgemeaning`](../claims/rem-edgemeaning.tex): Meaning of an edge ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:edgemeaning))

  **Non-normative summary.** An edge fixes the order in which two elements act; moving light between modes is done by the elements' matrices\. Components drawn on different arms carry no edge and have disjoint supports, so support comparability constrains only an ambiguous drawing\.

- 3.8 [`receiver/rem:noloops`](../claims/rem-noloops.tex): Feedback paths outside the acyclic scope ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:noloops))

  **Non-normative summary.** A cavity, a ring resonator or any other feedback path is a cycle, which \(E2\) excludes\. Compiling one would need a fixed\-point solve whose contractivity must itself be certified, so such a receiver needs a new claim rather than a larger computation\.

- 3.9 [`receiver/rem:acyc-efficiency`](../claims/rem-acyc-efficiency.tex): Where the element graph enters the efficiency theorem ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:acyc-efficiency))

  **Non-normative summary.** The claims that read only the Gram substitution of the efficiency theorem involve the terminal efficiency layer alone, a function of the port map and efficiencies, and no element graph\. The theorem's items on the compiled product and its record hold only for a well\-formed, hence acyclic, graph\.


### 4 Port Grams and the click-pattern POVM

[Dependency view](group-04-port-grams-and-the-click-pattern-povm.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The joint no\-click element of any set of ports pulls back to the lift of the identity minus the sum of the Grams of the ports in that set, whatever dilation is chosen \([Theorem “Port Grams and dilation independence”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:port-grams)\), and the loss deficiency splits exactly into loss through the dilation and light reaching unmonitored declared modes\. An outcome is the set of ports that click\. The main result, [Theorem “Click\-pattern POVM”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:click-povm), is the click\-pattern POVM: it follows by inclusion and exclusion, is positive by construction rather than by its alternating form, and is block diagonal in the total input photon number\.

- 4.1 [`receiver/thm:port-grams`](../claims/thm-port-grams.tex): Port Grams and dilation independence ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:port-grams))

  **Non-normative summary.** The port Grams form an admissible family, and every joint no\-click element pulls back to the lift of the identity minus the sum of the Grams of the ports in its set\. The measurement depends only on the declared input columns and on no choice of dilation\.

- 4.2 [`receiver/lem:deficiency`](../claims/lem-deficiency.tex): Decomposition of the loss deficiency ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:deficiency))

  **Non-normative summary.** The undetected fraction splits exactly into light lost through the dilation and light reaching declared modes that no port monitors, both positive semidefinite\. It is the check that loss is counted exactly once\.

- 4.3 [`receiver/rem:unmonitored`](../claims/rem-unmonitored.tex): Unmonitored output modes ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:unmonitored))

  **Non-normative summary.** Deleting the row of an output mode that no port monitors changes no port Gram and hence no POVM element; the deleted amplitude is carried by the dilation\. Which modes lie in a port is a declaration with no observable content beyond the Grams it produces\.

- 4.4 [`receiver/def:clickalphabet`](../claims/def-clickalphabet.tex): Click-pattern alphabet ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:clickalphabet))

  **Non-normative summary.** A raw outcome is the set of detector ports that click, and the raw alphabet splits into the no\-click outcome, the single\-click set and the multiclick set\. Any merging into a protocol's alphabet comes later\.

- 4.5 [`receiver/thm:click-povm`](../claims/thm-click-povm.tex): Click-pattern POVM ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:click-povm))

  **Non-normative summary.** The click patterns of a receiver instrument form a POVM whose elements follow from the port Grams by inclusion and exclusion\. Every element is block diagonal in the total input photon number, and vacuum gives only the no\-click outcome\.

- 4.6 [`receiver/rem:positivity`](../claims/rem-positivity.tex): Source of positivity of the click-pattern elements ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:positivity))

  **Non-normative summary.** The inclusion\-exclusion formula is an alternating sum and nothing in its form makes it positive\. Positivity comes first, from commuting projective measurements pulled back through an isometry, and the single\-click case also follows from monotonicity of the lift\.

- 4.7 [`receiver/rem:normordelements`](../claims/rem-normordelements.tex): Click-pattern elements in normal-ordered form ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:normordelements))

  **Non-normative summary.** Each joint no\-click element is the normal\-ordered exponential of the summed Gram A\_J of its ports, the form used in the optics literature\. For one port and one input mode with Gram A\_1 = 1 this is the perfect on/off detector pair of the cited literature\.

- 4.8 [`receiver/rem:small-k`](../claims/rem-small-k.tex): Small numbers of ports ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:small-k))

  **Non-normative summary.** With one port the multiclick element vanishes, as the closed form confirms; with two ports each single\-click element is the other port dark minus both dark\. These cases check the inclusion\-exclusion formula by hand\.

- 4.9 [`receiver/rem:blockmeaning`](../claims/rem-blockmeaning.tex): Scope of the photon-number block structure ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:blockmeaning))

  **Non-normative summary.** The block structure is in the total input photon number, which equals the channel photon number only without a probe; an occupation\-diagonal probe state suffices to recover the channel grading\. The emitted low blocks decompose the POVM and are not compressions or a squashing argument\.


### 5 Representation independence and coarse-graining

[Dependency view](group-05-representation-independence-and-coarse-graining.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Two receivers induce the same click POVM exactly when their port Grams agree, every admissible Gram family is realised by some instrument, and a one\-mode\-per\-port description exists exactly when every Gram has rank at most one \([Theorem “Representation independence of the click\-pattern POVM”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:representation-independence)\)\. Phases inside a single port after all interference are invisible; a phase on an earlier element whose output paths are later recombined is not covered and can change the Grams\. The main result, [Theorem “Coarse\-graining of the click\-pattern POVM”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:coarse-graining), is that under a declared coarse\-graining, which merges raw outcomes into the protocol's alphabet, lower bounds on disjoint raw outcomes add, and a bound on a merged outcome gives nothing about its parts\.

- 5.1 [`receiver/thm:representation-independence`](../claims/thm-representation-independence.tex): Representation independence of the click-pattern POVM ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:representation-independence))

  **Non-normative summary.** Two receivers have the same click\-pattern POVM exactly when their port Grams agree, every admissible Gram family is realised by some instrument, and a one\-mode\-per\-port description exists exactly when every Gram has rank at most one\.

- 5.2 [`receiver/cor:phases`](../claims/cor-phases.tex): Unobservability of per-port phases ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/cor:phases))

  **Non-normative summary.** A unitary acting inside a single detector port after all interference changes no click statistic\. A phase inserted earlier, on an element whose output paths are later recombined, is not covered by this invariance\.

- 5.3 [`receiver/cor:extinction`](../claims/cor-extinction.tex): Extinction criterion for rank one at two input modes ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/cor:extinction))

  **Non-normative summary.** At two input modes a nonzero port Gram has rank one exactly when the port extinguishes some polarization completely\. At more input modes one null direction bounds the rank only by the number of input modes minus one\.

- 5.4 [`receiver/rem:rankone`](../claims/rem-rankone.tex): Ports of rank greater than one ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:rankone))

  **Non-normative summary.** No result of this contract assumes one optical mode per detector port: a port collecting several modes can have a Gram of rank above one\. The one\-mode\-per\-port description holds exactly under the rank criterion of [Theorem “Representation independence of the click\-pattern POVM”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:representation-independence) \(iv\)\.

- 5.5 [`receiver/def:coarse`](../claims/def-coarse.tex): Declared coarse-graining and the emitted POVM ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:coarse))

  **Non-normative summary.** A declared surjection from raw click patterns onto the protocol's outcome alphabet defines the emitted measurement\. Each emitted element is the sum of the click\-pattern elements in its fibre\.

- 5.6 [`receiver/thm:coarse-graining`](../claims/thm-coarse-graining.tex): Coarse-graining of the click-pattern POVM ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:coarse-graining))

  **Non-normative summary.** The emitted POVM of a declared coarse\-graining is a POVM, block diagonal in the photon number, and lower bounds on disjoint raw outcomes add\. A bound on a merged outcome gives nothing about the click patterns it merges\.


### 6 The analytic high-photon bound

[Dependency view](group-06-the-analytic-high-photon-bound.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A squashing argument needs a lower bound on the multiclick element valid on every photon\-number block above the cutoff\. Extreme eigenvalues of the no\-click operators give a scalar bound function on each block \([Theorem “Lower bound on every multiclick block”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:multiclick-bound)\); it is not monotone in general\. Where its value at zero photons is at most zero, the bound cannot decrease from any photon number n ≥ 1 at which it is non\-negative, so the non\-negative part of the infimum over the tail is that of its value at the first photon number above the cutoff \([Lemma “Collapse of the tail infimum to the first photon number”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:collapse)\); otherwise a finite window with an analytic tail gives a valid bound that converges to the infimum when c \< 1, with a stated residual gap \([Lemma “Window\-and\-tail bound on the tail infimum”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:windowtail)\)\.

- 6.1 [`receiver/def:bounddata`](../claims/def-bounddata.tex): Admissible bound data and the bound function ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:bounddata))

  **Non-normative summary.** Ordered scalars between 0 and 1, one pair shared and one pair per port, define an exponential bound function of the photon number for the multiclick element\. Admissibility is exact, and distinct from the tolerance rules that admit a floating\-point evaluation\.

- 6.2 [`receiver/thm:multiclick-bound`](../claims/thm-multiclick-bound.tex): Lower bound on every multiclick block ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:multiclick-bound))

  **Non-normative summary.** For no\-click operators of prefactor form, every n\-photon block of the multiclick combination is bounded below by the bound function at n, built from extreme eigenvalues of the mode operators B and Bₖ\. The bound is at most one\.

- 6.3 [`receiver/rem:k-minus-1`](../claims/rem-k-minus-1.tex): The coefficient K-1 of the all-dark element ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:k-minus-1))

  **Non-normative summary.** The all\-dark element \(no port clicks\) enters the multiclick element with coefficient K−1: each of the K single\-click elements contributes one, and the no\-click element, which is this same operator, takes one away\. An error by one would show only where c \> 0\.

- 6.4 [`receiver/rem:kamin`](../claims/rem-kamin.tex): Relation to a published multiclick weight bound ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:kamin))

  **Non-normative summary.** A published construction bounds a photon\-number subspace weight from an observed multiclick rate, for one row per detector\. [Theorem “Lower bound on every multiclick block”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:multiclick-bound) is instead an operator inequality for every input state and ports of any rank; no step here depends on the citation\.

- 6.5 [`receiver/lem:collapse`](../claims/lem-collapse.tex): Collapse of the tail infimum to the first photon number ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:collapse))

  **Non-normative summary.** If the bound function is at most zero at zero photons, it cannot decrease from any n ≥ 1 at which it is non\-negative\. The non\-negative part of its infimum above a cutoff is then that of its value at the next photon number\.

- 6.6 [`receiver/rem:n0`](../claims/rem-n0.tex): The monotonicity step at zero photons ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:n0))

  **Non-normative summary.** The monotonicity step of [Lemma “Collapse of the tail infimum to the first photon number”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:collapse) fails at n = 0, with an explicit two\-port receiver as witness\. Nothing is lost, because the infimum runs over photon numbers above the cutoff, all at least one\.

- 6.7 [`receiver/rem:collapsesharp`](../claims/rem-collapsesharp.tex): Sharpness of the collapse lemma ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:collapsesharp))

  **Non-normative summary.** At the data γ = βₖ = 1, d₁ = 1 and dₖ = c for k ≥ 2, the bound function is identically zero, so the monotonicity step of [Lemma “Collapse of the tail infimum to the first photon number”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:collapse) cannot be strengthened to strict increase\.

- 6.8 [`receiver/lem:windowtail`](../claims/lem-windowtail.tex): Window-and-tail bound on the tail infimum ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:windowtail))

  **Non-normative summary.** The minimum of the bound function over a finite window, capped by an analytic tail term, is a valid uniform bound above the cutoff\. It improves as the window widens, converges to the infimum when c \< 1, and a residual gap bounds what remains\.


### 7 Exact block eigenvalues

[Dependency view](group-07-exact-block-eigenvalues.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The analytic bound bounds three operators separately and loses slack that no wider window recovers\. Computing the smallest eigenvalue of a block itself recovers it: that exact block bound dominates the analytic bound, is at most one, and is non\-negative wherever the multiclick element is positive \([Lemma “Range of the exact block bound”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:exactbasic)\)\. Lower estimates on finitely many exact blocks combine with an admissible analytic remainder into a uniform bound over the whole tail \([Lemma “Exact window with an analytic remainder”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:exactwindow)\)\.

- 7.1 [`receiver/def:exactbnd`](../claims/def-exactbnd.tex): The exact block bound function ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:exactbnd))

  **Non-normative summary.** The exact block bound β\*\(n\) is the smallest eigenvalue of the n\-photon block of the multiclick element\. Computing it on finitely many blocks recovers the slack the analytic bound loses by bounding three operators separately\.

- 7.2 [`receiver/lem:exactbasic`](../claims/lem-exactbasic.tex): Range of the exact block bound ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:exactbasic))

  **Non-normative summary.** The exact block bound lies between the analytic bound and one on every photon\-number block, and it is non\-negative when the multiclick element is positive\. That positivity holds at every setting of a setting family but does not follow from the multiclick bound's hypotheses\.

- 7.3 [`receiver/rem:exactpositivity`](../claims/rem-exactpositivity.tex): Positivity of the multiclick element as a separate hypothesis ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:exactpositivity))

  **Non-normative summary.** Data satisfying every hypothesis of the multiclick bound can display a multiclick element with exact block bound −3, so non\-negativity of the exact block bound needs positivity as its own hypothesis\. No receiver of this contract produces such an operator\.

- 7.4 [`receiver/lem:exactwindow`](../claims/lem-exactwindow.tex): Exact window with an analytic remainder ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:exactwindow))

  **Non-normative summary.** Lower estimates of the exact block bound on finitely many blocks above the cutoff, combined with an admissible remainder for the blocks beyond them, give a weight in \[0,1\] that bounds the multiclick element on every block above the cutoff\. Three admissible choices are named\.

- 7.5 [`receiver/rem:exactscope`](../claims/rem-exactscope.tex): Scope of the exact-window lemma ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/rem:exactscope))

  **Non-normative summary.** The exact\-window lemma makes finitely many blocks yield a bound uniform above the cutoff, from remainders already proved, and uses positivity at one step only\. It is stated on the exact block bound and makes no computed eigenvalue available by itself\.


### 8 Setting families and the emitted weight

[Dependency view](group-08-setting-families-and-the-emitted-weight.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A setting family packages, for each declared setting, a photon\-number block\-diagonal POVM whose joint no\-click elements have the prefactor form of the multiclick bound; a probe\-free receiver is the one\-setting family, and a submodule that constructs another family proves there that it is one\. Where a depth is declared, a setting's weight is an exact window; elsewhere it is the collapse value where the declaration marks the setting exact and a declared window and tail otherwise; their minimum is the uniform weight\. Formed on bounds that lie on the conservative side of the exact data, the same formulas give the enclosed weight, a bound with no assumption \([Theorem “The weight on enclosed bound data bounds the multiclick element”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:enclosed-weight)\)\. The first main result, [Theorem “Multiclick bounds carried by the emitted weights”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:emitted-bound), is that each per\-setting weight bounds its setting's multiclick element on every block above the cutoff, and the uniform weight bounds it at every setting, under the eigenvalue\-slack assumption only where a computed block eigenvalue enters\. The second, [Proposition “Range of the strengthening\-slot weights”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/prop:slotvalid), is that the strengthening slot in which the artifact carries the weights has weights that are non\-negative and sum to at most one at every setting, as a squashing request requires\.

  **Non-normative summary.** A setting family gives, per setting, a photon\-number block\-diagonal POVM whose joint no\-click elements have the prefactor form of the multiclick bound, and a flag that reads exact only where every prefactor is one by construction\. A depth changes only its own setting's weight\.

### 9 The reference, enclosed and exact

[Dependency view](group-09-the-reference-enclosed-and-exact.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The reference a run hands beside the snap of the emitted blocks, and evaluates its first\-order bound on, must be known through rational boxes, and the binary64 evaluation is not one\. Complex enclosures, a Gaussian\-rational centre with a rational radius, are carried through sums, products and reciprocals by explicit rules \([Lemma “Arithmetic on complex enclosures”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:enclosure-arithmetic)\)\. An entry of a lifted operator in the occupation basis is a permanent divided by square roots of factorials \([Lemma “Entries of a lifted operator in the occupation basis”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:fock-permanent)\), so enclosures of the port Grams, themselves obtained from enclosures of square roots, cosines and sines, enclose every block, and the emitted blocks up to the cutoff are a reference for any declared parameters \([Theorem “Rigorous enclosures of the photon\-number blocks”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:enclosed-blocks)\)\. Where the port Grams are Gaussian\-rational, the exact route \([Definition “Gaussian\-rational port Grams and the exact route”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:exact-route)\), every block of at most one photon is known exactly, which a snap needs in order to keep a block unmoved \([Theorem “Exact entries of the blocks on the exact route”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:exact-blocks)\)\. Read through a port dilation, an isometry from the inputs that reproduces the port Grams \([Definition “Port dilation of a receiver instrument”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:port-dilation)\), every block is also a weighted sum of rank\-one terms whose columns are enclosed the same way, a factor enclosure, which fixes the block's rank where those columns are certified independent \([Theorem “Photon\-number blocks in enclosed factor form”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:factor-blocks)\)\.

- 9.1 [`receiver/def:complex-enclosure`](../claims/def-complex-enclosure.tex): Complex enclosures, and enclosures of square roots and angles ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:complex-enclosure))

  **Non-normative summary.** A complex enclosure of a number is a Gaussian\-rational centre with a rational radius that bounds its distance from the number; a matrix is enclosed entry by entry\. Square roots, cosines and sines are enclosed by any such pair with a rational centre, exactly where the value is plainly rational\.

- 9.2 [`receiver/lem:enclosure-arithmetic`](../claims/lem-enclosure-arithmetic.tex): Arithmetic on complex enclosures ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:enclosure-arithmetic))

  **Non-normative summary.** Sums, products, conjugates and reciprocals of enclosed numbers are enclosed by explicit rational rules, so any finite expression in enclosed numbers is enclosed and stays exact where its inputs are\. Averaging a Hermitian matrix's enclosure with its adjoint makes the centre Hermitian\.

- 9.3 [`receiver/lem:fock-permanent`](../claims/lem-fock-permanent.tex): Entries of a lifted operator in the occupation basis ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:fock-permanent))

  **Non-normative summary.** An entry of the n\-photon block of a lifted operator is a permanent of a submatrix with repeated rows and columns, divided by the square roots of two products of factorials\. For a Gaussian\-rational operator the entry is Gaussian\-rational on the diagonal and on every block of at most one photon\.

- 9.4 [`receiver/lem:fock-permanent-maps`](../claims/lem-fock-permanent-maps.tex): The lift of a map between two mode sets ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:fock-permanent-maps))

  **Non-normative summary.** The Fock lift of a linear map between two mode sets respects composition and adjoints and keeps an isometry an isometry, and its entries in the occupation bases are permanents divided by square roots of factorials\. Reading a block through a port dilation needs both\.

- 9.5 [`receiver/thm:enclosed-blocks`](../claims/thm-enclosed-blocks.tex): Rigorous enclosures of the photon-number blocks ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:enclosed-blocks))

  **Non-normative summary.** Enclosures of the port Grams, obtained from enclosures of square roots, cosines and sines, carry through permanents, inclusion and exclusion and the coarse\-graining to enclosures of every block\. With Hermitian centres they make the emitted blocks up to any cutoff a reference\.

- 9.6 [`receiver/def:port-dilation`](../claims/def-port-dilation.tex): Port dilation of a receiver instrument ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:port-dilation))

  **Non-normative summary.** A port dilation is an isometry from the declared inputs into a finite mode set with disjoint ports that reproduces every port Gram\. Any such map carries the click statistics as the unitary dilation of the physical model does, and it may have far fewer modes\.

- 9.7 [`receiver/thm:factor-blocks`](../claims/thm-factor-blocks.tex): Photon-number blocks in enclosed factor form ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/thm:factor-blocks))

  **Non-normative summary.** Read through a port dilation, a non\-negative combination of click\-pattern blocks is a weighted sum of rank\-one terms whose columns are permanents of the dilation, enclosed by the same rules as the blocks\. ## Part III: Component catalogue

**Non-normative summary.** The component catalogue lets a receiver be declared as an optical layout instead of a single matrix\. Each of its ten entries fixes a support, a parameter domain and a passive matrix with every phase and every loss the part has written out \([Definition “Entries of the component catalogue”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/component-catalogue/def:catalogue-entries)\)\. The main result is that every entry maps its whole declared domain into local elements, lossless exactly on a named subfamily \([Theorem “Admissibility of the catalogue entries”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/component-catalogue/thm:catalogue-admissibility)\); one attenuation lemma carries most of the proof\. The amplitude each entry removes has rows of its own, none on the lossless subfamily, and along a compiled graph they make a port dilation of the receiver \([Lemma “Loss factors of the catalogue entries and the dilation of a graph”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/component-catalogue/lem:catalogue-dilations)\)\. A worked BB84 receiver follows one declaration through compilation to its port Grams, its analytic high\-photon weight and the improvement an exact window buys at a smaller cutoff\.

## Part IV: Detector imperfections

**Non-normative summary.** This submodule adds detector efficiency and dark counts to the passive receiver, and its two main results say how each acts on the measurement\. Efficiency uniform within a port scales that port's Gram and enters at the single terminal layer of the compiled matrix \([Theorem “Detector efficiency in the port Grams”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/thm:efficiency)\); efficiency that varies within a port is not a scalar and can lower a Gram's rank\. Dark counts are a classical channel on the recorded click pattern that adds clicks and never removes them\. Such a channel preserves the POVM structure and improves the multiclick bound p\_mc to \(1 − r\_min\)p\_mc \+ r\_min, r\_min being the least favourable transition from every non\-multiclick outcome, the no\-click outcome included \([Theorem “Dark counts on a photon\-number block\-diagonal POVM”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/thm:dark-counts)\)\. A declared channel is admitted at two receiver tolerances, and the bound a downstream module composes with carries their cost explicitly\. The emitted blocks are those of the dark\-composed family, enclosed rigorously for any declared parameters and exact where the enclosures have radius zero; loss and a dark count in every port make each of its elements positive definite on every block, and neither can be dropped \([Proposition “Loss and dark counts make every dark\-composed element definite”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:noise-floor)\), which is the floor a model needs if no snap of its blocks is to revive a zero row\. A receiver without a probe is a uniform loss of transmittance t followed by a normalized station whose efficiencies are divided by t, for any t from its largest efficiency η\_max to one, so that loss can be handed to the adversary with the observed statistics unchanged \([Proposition “A receiver as uniform loss followed by its normalized station”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:efficiency-normalization)\); by default the engine compiles the normalized station at t = η\_max/\(99/100\), and the declared receiver \(t = 1\) where η\_max ≥ 99/100\.

### 18 Detector efficiency

[Dependency view](group-18-detector-imperfections-detector-efficiency.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The main result, [Theorem “Detector efficiency in the port Grams”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/thm:efficiency), is that detector efficiency is a parameter of the terminal layer, applied after every chain element, that multiplies each port Gram by its efficiency when uniform within the port\. The remarks show a within\-port attenuation that lowers a rank, why a product of transmittances is the wrong loss audit in an interferometer, and why power data and click data differ by exactly the efficiencies\.

- 18.1 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/thm:efficiency`](../detector-imperfections/claims/thm-efficiency.tex): Detector efficiency in the port Grams ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/thm:efficiency))

  **Non-normative summary.** Efficiency η\_k uniform on detector port k scales that port's Gram by η\_k, keeping its rank if η\_k \> 0; varying within a port, it is no scalar and can lower the rank\. It enters only as the compiled instrument matrix's last layer, and the loss decomposition [Lemma “Decomposition of the loss deficiency”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/lem:deficiency) checks that loss is counted once\.

- 18.2 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:effrank`](../detector-imperfections/claims/rem-effrank.tex): A port Gram whose rank drops under within-port attenuation ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:effrank))

  **Non-normative summary.** A port of two modes with Gram diag\(1, ε²\) has rank two; attenuating one of its modes to zero leaves rank one\. Apart from a zero efficiency, within\-port attenuation is the one operation in this contract that can change the answer to the one\-mode\-per\-port rank test\.

- 18.3 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:wrongcheck`](../detector-imperfections/claims/rem-wrongcheck.tex): Failure of the path-product loss audit under interference ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:wrongcheck))

  **Non-normative summary.** In a lossless balanced Mach–Zehnder interferometer at zero phase, a product of transmittances along paths reports a spurious loss of one half at the first port, as it cannot see the interference cross term\. The deficiency identity is the audit that holds, and the artifact reports it as a residual\.

- 18.4 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:powervsclick`](../detector-imperfections/claims/rem-powervsclick.tex): Power data and click data measure different objects ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:powervsclick))

  **Non-normative summary.** A power characterisation of the optics returns the port Grams A\_k, click statistics return η\_k A\_k\. Double counting is avoided exactly when the efficiencies enter once, and which convention the declared numbers follow cannot be read off the numbers\.

- 18.5 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:sperlingeta`](../detector-imperfections/claims/rem-sperlingeta.tex): The single-mode efficiency substitution in the photo-counting formula ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:sperlingeta))

  **Non-normative summary.** For one mode the efficiency substitution gives an on/off detector of efficiency η the normally ordered no\-click element exp\(−ηn\), the zero\-count, noise\-free case of a published photo\-counting formula\. The agreement corroborates and backs no step\.


### 19 Dark counts

[Dependency view](group-19-detector-imperfections-dark-counts.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A dark\-count channel is column\-stochastic and monotone\. The main result, [Theorem “Dark counts on a photon\-number block\-diagonal POVM”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/thm:dark-counts), is that at every setting it keeps the composed family a photon\-number block\-diagonal POVM, never moves weight out of the multiclick set, and improves the multiclick bound; the minimum defining the improvement must run over the no\-click column too\. A declared channel is admitted at two receiver tolerances, and the composed inequality that holds for every admitted channel carries both slacks \([Proposition “Multiclick bound under a dark\-count channel admitted with slack”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:dark-slack-bound)\)\.

- 19.1 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/def:darkadm`](../detector-imperfections/claims/def-darkadm.tex): Dark-count channel and its independent product form ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/def:darkadm))

  **Non-normative summary.** A dark\-count channel is a column\-stochastic matrix on click patterns that can add clicks and never removes one\. It is generated from per\-port dark\-count probabilities in product form, or declared as a matrix and then checked at the receiver tolerances\.

- 19.2 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/thm:dark-counts`](../detector-imperfections/claims/thm-dark-counts.tex): Dark counts on a photon-number block-diagonal POVM ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/thm:dark-counts))

  **Non-normative summary.** A dark\-count channel keeps the photon\-number block\-diagonal POVM structure and never moves weight out of the multiclick set\. It raises the multiclick bound p\_mc to \(1 − r\_min\)p\_mc \+ r\_min, where r\_min is the least multiclick mass over every non\-multiclick column, the no\-click column included\.

- 19.3 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/def:darktol`](../detector-imperfections/claims/def-darktol.tex): Receiver tolerances deciding dark-channel admissibility ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/def:darktol))

  **Non-normative summary.** For a declared dark\-count matrix, column\-stochasticity is decided at ε\_4 and monotonicity at ε\_2\. This is item \(iii\) of [Definition “Receiver tolerances and the exact conditions they decide”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/def:tolmap); the slack the two tests admit is carried into the bound by [Proposition “Multiclick bound under a dark\-count channel admitted with slack”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:dark-slack-bound)\.

- 19.4 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:dark-slack-bound`](../detector-imperfections/claims/prop-dark-slack-bound.tex): Multiclick bound under a dark-count channel admitted with slack ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:dark-slack-bound))

  **Non-normative summary.** For a declared dark\-count matrix admitted at ε\_2 and ε\_4, each multiclick column keeps mass at least 1 − δ\_dc, r\_min can exceed one by at most \(K\+2\)ε\_4, and the dark\-count improvement holds with rbar = min\{r\_min, 1\} and δ\_dc subtracted\. This is the inequality a downstream module composes with\.

- 19.5 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:rmintrap`](../detector-imperfections/claims/rem-rmintrap.tex): The no-click column in the minimum defining r_min ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:rmintrap))

  **Non-normative summary.** Taking r\_min over the single\-click columns only gives an invalid bound whenever the no\-click column has less multiclick mass, the generic case: at K = 4 and p = 0\.00001 the two differ by four orders of magnitude\. A lossy receiver keeps no\-click weight in every high\-photon block\.

- 19.6 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:d2slack`](../detector-imperfections/claims/rem-d2slack.tex): Cost of admitting a dark-count channel with slack ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:d2slack))

  **Non-normative summary.** Admitting a declared channel at ε\_2 and ε\_4 relaxes a hypothesis, not a precision; r\_min above one is reachable, and ignoring it or δ\_dc overstates the multiclick bound in the unsafe direction\. The composed family is a POVM only up to ε\_4, a defect left visible and not repaired\.

- 19.7 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:corrdark`](../detector-imperfections/claims/rem-corrdark.tex): Correlated dark counts within a round ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:corrdark))

  **Non-normative summary.** A declared dark\-count channel need not have product form: if it satisfies \(D1\) and \(D2\) exactly, the dark\-count theorem holds for it verbatim, and if it passes the widened tests it holds in the weakened forms of [Proposition “Multiclick bound under a dark\-count channel admitted with slack”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:dark-slack-bound) and [Remark “Cost of admitting a dark\-count channel with slack”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:d2slack)\. Correlations across rounds are excluded\.


### 20 The dark-composed family and the noise floor

[Dependency view](group-20-detector-imperfections-the-dark-composed-family-and-the-noise-floor.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The dark\-composed family applies the dark\-count channel to every setting's click patterns and then the coarse\-graining \([Definition “The dark\-composed family”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/def:dark-composed)\)\. A dark\-count channel has rational entries and carries enclosures of the click\-pattern blocks to enclosures of the dark\-composed ones, which form the reference handed beside the snap \([Corollary “The dark\-composed reference, enclosed or exact”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/cor:exact-dark-composed)\)\. The main point of the section is [Proposition “Loss and dark counts make every dark\-composed element definite”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:noise-floor): loss and a dark\-count probability in \(0, 1\) in every port make every dark\-composed element positive definite on every photon\-number block, with an explicit floor, and without either some element is singular\. The closing remark says what a declaration should therefore model, why an ideal device measuring in a rotated basis dominates no lattice family beyond one photon at a small weight \(for the two\-photon block of the passive BB84 receiver at a balanced basis choice, 1/4\), and why a run at a cutoff of two or more photons that uses no enclosed reference typically rests on its snap\.

- 20.1 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/def:dark-composed`](../detector-imperfections/claims/def-dark-composed.tex): The dark-composed family ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/def:dark-composed))

  **Non-normative summary.** The dark\-composed family applies a dark\-count channel to the click patterns of each setting's POVM and then the declared coarse\-graining\. It is the measurement the device performs, and the family a snap is taken from\.

- 20.2 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/cor:exact-dark-composed`](../detector-imperfections/claims/cor-exact-dark-composed.tex): The dark-composed reference, enclosed or exact ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/cor:exact-dark-composed))

  **Non-normative summary.** A dark\-count channel has rational entries and mixes whole elements with non\-negative weights, so enclosures of the click\-pattern blocks give enclosures of the dark\-composed blocks, exact where theirs are\. With Hermitian centres they make the dark\-composed blocks up to the cutoff a reference\.

- 20.3 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:noise-floor`](../detector-imperfections/claims/prop-noise-floor.tex): Loss and dark counts make every dark-composed element definite ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:noise-floor))

  **Non-normative summary.** With loss and a dark\-count probability in \(0,1\) in every port, every dark\-composed element is positive definite on every photon\-number block, with a least eigenvalue bounded below through the no\-click element\. Without loss, or without a dark count in some port, some element is singular\.

- 20.4 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:noise-floor-receiver`](../detector-imperfections/claims/rem-noise-floor-receiver.tex): What a receiver declaration needs for a rate that rests on its model ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:noise-floor-receiver))

  **Non-normative summary.** Declare the loss and dark counts the device surely has, in every port; then every element is positive definite and no snap revives a zero row\. A run that uses no enclosed reference rests, at a cutoff of two or more, on its snap; an angle decides whether the reference can be exact\.


### 21 Efficiency normalization

[Dependency view](group-21-detector-imperfections-efficiency-normalization.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A common factor of the port Grams is a uniform loss that commutes with every passive element, so it can be moved in front of the receiver and counted as part of the channel\. The section defines the pure\-loss channel and the normalized station, whose efficiencies are the receiver's divided by a factored loss t, and its main point is [Proposition “A receiver as uniform loss followed by its normalized station”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:efficiency-normalization): every click pattern of the receiver, and every dark\-count composition of them, is the pull\-back of the normalized station's through the pure\-loss channel of transmittance t\. The remarks show that at low efficiency the normalization removes a squared\-efficiency factor from the two\-photon multiclick block, and say what it leaves in place, efficiency mismatch included\.

- 21.1 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/def:pure-loss`](../detector-imperfections/claims/def-pure-loss.tex): Pure-loss channel on the channel modes ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/def:pure-loss))

  **Non-normative summary.** The pure\-loss channel of transmittance t sends every channel mode through a beamsplitter of transmittance t whose other input is vacuum and discards the reflected light\. It is the uniform loss a normalized station hands to the channel\.

- 21.2 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/def:normalized-station`](../detector-imperfections/claims/def-normalized-station.tex): Normalized station and factored loss ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/def:normalized-station))

  **Non-normative summary.** A normalized station at factor t divides every detector efficiency of a probe\-free receiver by t, for t between the largest efficiency and one; t is the factored loss\. Its port Grams are those of the receiver divided by t\.

- 21.3 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/lem:pure-loss-lift`](../detector-imperfections/claims/lem-pure-loss-lift.tex): The pure-loss channel on the lift of a contraction ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/lem:pure-loss-lift))

  **Non-normative summary.** The adjoint of the pure\-loss channel of transmittance t takes the lift of a contraction X to the lift of tX \+ \(1 − t\)·1\. Every joint no\-click element is such a lift, so loss in front of a receiver acts on its Grams as a scalar\.

- 21.4 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:efficiency-normalization`](../detector-imperfections/claims/prop-efficiency-normalization.tex): A receiver as uniform loss followed by its normalized station ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/prop:efficiency-normalization))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every click pattern of a probe\-free receiver is the pull\-back, through the pure\-loss channel of the factored loss t, of the same pattern of its normalized station, and so is every dark\-count composition\. The statistics agree, and the loss can be handed to the adversary\.

- 21.5 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:normalization-gain`](../detector-imperfections/claims/rem-normalization-gain.tex): The two-photon multiclick block scales with the squared efficiency ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:normalization-gain))

  **Non-normative summary.** At a common efficiency η and no dark counts, the two\-photon multiclick block of a receiver is η² times that of its normalized station, so every multiclick weight it can emit at a cutoff of one photon is at most η²\. Normalization removes that factor without enlarging the adversary's reach\.

- 21.6 [`receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:normalization-scope`](../detector-imperfections/claims/rem-normalization-scope.tex): What the normalization factors out and what it leaves in place ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-receiver/detector-imperfections/rem:normalization-scope))

  **Non-normative summary.** Only the per\-port efficiencies are divided: declared mode\-dependent loss stays, undeclared efficiency mismatch is outside the assumptions either way, and a probe\-fed receiver is not covered\. The full normalization can remove the loss the noise floor needs, which a larger factor keeps\.
