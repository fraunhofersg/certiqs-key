## Module summary

Summaries explain purpose and content. They are non-normative; the claim statements, proofs and declared assumptions remain authoritative.

**Non-normative summary.** This module builds, before the run and from a declared honest model, the acceptance set against which a protocol's observed outcome frequencies are tested, and states how often an honest run passes that test; the key\-rate engine of the meat\-core module reads the set as the acceptance set of the protocol declaration it solves\. Its core fixes the frequency vector of a run and the two admissible geometries, a single frequency vector or a rational polytope in the probability simplex, the polytope possibly in canonical box form with recorded bounds per outcome\. The core's main results are that every geometry passing its validation predicate is a non\-empty, compact and convex subset of the simplex, as the engine's acceptance assumption requires \([Theorem “Validity of the admissible geometries”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/thm:geometry-validation)\), and that over a non\-empty canonical box, filling the coordinates of smallest coefficient first minimises a rational linear functional exactly, with a dual witness of the same value in the format the engine's certificate expects, while the outcomes with a positive upper bound include every outcome on which a point of the box puts weight \([Theorem “Acceptance support and the exact box oracle”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/thm:box-oracle)\)\. The core also supplies the binomial tails in closed form and the honest envelope, a per\-outcome range of honest probabilities of which one honest law is the degenerate case\. The binomial\-box submodule constructs the box and bounds the probability that an honest run fails the test; the singleton submodule evaluates a one\-point test exactly\. The declared premises are the honest\-round model, an acceptance value recorded in every round, an honest law that some feasible state of the engine's declaration produces, and the output\-state model of the test statistics \([A\-TSM](../../reductions/contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-reductions/ass:tsm)\), under which the acceptance outcome carries every per\-round public choice of known bias made at a measuring station, a letter\-side choice being a component of the letter drawn as declared \([A\-COIN](../../protocol-compiler/contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-protocol-compiler/ass:coin)\); that the alphabet and round count are those of that declaration, and that the data are exact and the tails conservatively evaluated, are conditions the engine decides\. The honest\-round model and the premise on that feasible state bear on completeness, rate and usability, never on the soundness of a key\.

## Part I: Mathematics

### 1 Frequencies and acceptance geometries

[Dependency view](group-01-frequencies-and-acceptance-geometries.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A run's outcome counts divided by the round count give a frequency vector in the probability simplex, and the acceptance test asks whether that vector lies in a declared set\. The alphabet and the round count are those of the declaration the consuming engine solves\. The set is an admissible geometry, a single frequency vector or a rational polytope in the simplex, the polytope possibly in canonical box form with recorded lower and upper bounds per outcome\. The main result, [Theorem “Validity of the admissible geometries”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/thm:geometry-validation), is that a geometry passing its validation predicate is a non\-empty, compact and convex subset of the simplex; for the canonical box form the predicate is a closed\-form condition on the sums of the bounds\.

- 1.1 [`statistics/def:freq`](../claims/def-freq.tex): Frequency vector and counts ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/def:freq))

  **Non-normative summary.** An outcome's count is the number of rounds in which it occurs, and the frequency vector of a run of n rounds is its counts divided by n\. The acceptance test asks whether this vector lies in the acceptance set\.

- 1.2 [`statistics/lem:freq-in-simplex`](../claims/lem-freq-in-simplex.tex): Frequency vectors in the probability simplex ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/lem:freq-in-simplex))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every frequency vector has non\-negative coordinates summing to one, since each round contributes exactly one outcome\. Intersecting a constraint set with the probability simplex therefore never changes the probability that a run passes the test, so simplex constraints cost no completeness\.

- 1.3 [`statistics/def:matched-alphabet`](../claims/def-matched-alphabet.tex): Alphabet and round count matched to the consuming declaration ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/def:matched-alphabet))

  **Non-normative summary.** An alphabet and a round count are matched when the alphabet, with a fixed enumeration, is the acceptance alphabet of the declaration the consumer solves and the round count is the same\. The acceptance set then lives on the consumer's coordinates and is normalised by its round count\.

- 1.4 [`statistics/def:taxonomy`](../claims/def-taxonomy.tex): Admissible geometries and the canonical box form ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/def:taxonomy))

  **Non-normative summary.** An admissible geometry is a singleton, one rational vector, or a polytope, rational linear inequalities intersected with the probability simplex, each with its validation predicate\. A polytope in canonical box form is given by recorded lower and upper bounds per outcome\.

- 1.5 [`statistics/thm:geometry-validation`](../claims/thm-geometry-validation.tex): Validity of the admissible geometries ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/thm:geometry-validation))

  **Non-normative summary.** An acceptance set whose data pass validation \(a probability vector, or a feasible linear system\) is a non\-empty, compact, admissible acceptance set \([Definition “Admissible acceptance set”](../../meat-core/contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-meat-core/def:admissible-acceptance-set)\)\. For a box of per\-outcome bounds, validation means the lower sum to at most one and the upper to at least one\.

- 1.6 [`statistics/rem:two-tags`](../claims/rem-two-tags.tex): The two tags and the consumer's certified geometries ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/rem:two-tags))

  **Non-normative summary.** The two tags are exactly the two geometries over which the consuming engine certifies the minimum of a linear functional, and the canonical box form is a sub\-form of the polytope, not a third tag\. A consumer that ignores the recorded bounds still reads a well\-formed polytope\.


### 2 Binomial tails

[Dependency view](group-02-binomial-tails.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The count of one outcome over independent rounds with one law is binomial, and the box construction reads its two tails\. They are regularised incomplete beta integrals, with every boundary case of the count stated and the upper tail non\-decreasing in the success probability \([Theorem “Binomial tails in closed form”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/thm:binomial-tails)\)\. The two strict tails the construction compares with a budget follow by a shift of the count \([Corollary “The two strict binomial tails”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/cor:tail-forms)\), and at a rational success probability both can be decided exactly \([Lemma “Exact strict tails at a rational success probability”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/lem:exact-strict-tails)\)\.

- 2.1 [`statistics/thm:binomial-tails`](../claims/thm-binomial-tails.tex): Binomial tails in closed form ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/thm:binomial-tails))

  **Non-normative summary.** The upper and lower tails of a binomial count are regularised incomplete beta integrals, with every boundary case of the count stated\. The upper tail is non\-decreasing in the success probability\.

- 2.2 [`statistics/cor:tail-forms`](../claims/cor-tail-forms.tex): The two strict binomial tails ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/cor:tail-forms))

  **Non-normative summary.** For every count k from 0 to n the strict tails of a binomial count N, the probabilities that N lies below k and above k, are regularised incomplete beta integrals, zero at k = 0 and at k = n respectively\. These are the two tails the endpoint rules compare with their budgets\.

- 2.3 [`statistics/lem:exact-strict-tails`](../claims/lem-exact-strict-tails.tex): Exact strict tails at a rational success probability ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/lem:exact-strict-tails))

  **Non-normative summary.** At a rational success probability both strict tails of a binomial count are finite sums of binomial probabilities, hence rational numbers, and each comparison of a tail with a rational budget is decided exactly\. The endpoint rules can therefore be evaluated without rounding\.


### 3 The exact box oracle

[Dependency view](group-03-the-exact-box-oracle.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The consuming engine minimises linear functionals over the acceptance set and needs to know on which outcomes an accepted frequency can put weight\. The main result, [Theorem “Acceptance support and the exact box oracle”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/thm:box-oracle), solves the minimisation over a non\-empty canonical box exactly by filling the coordinates of smallest coefficient first, from their lower towards their upper bounds, supplies a dual witness of the same value in the format the engine's certificate expects, and shows that the outcomes with a positive upper bound include every outcome on which a point of the box puts weight, and can include more\.

- 3.1 [`statistics/thm:box-oracle`](../claims/thm-box-oracle.tex): Acceptance support and the exact box oracle ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/thm:box-oracle))

  **Non-normative summary.** Over a non\-empty canonical box, filling the coordinates of smallest coefficient first minimises a rational linear functional exactly, with a dual witness of the same value\. The outcomes with a positive upper bound include every outcome on which a point of the box puts weight, and can include more\.

- 3.2 [`statistics/rem:box-oracle-consumer`](../claims/rem-box-oracle-consumer.tex): The closed-form box minimum for the consuming engine ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/rem:box-oracle-consumer))

  **Non-normative summary.** Over the canonical box the minimum of a linear functional that the consuming engine certifies is computable exactly in closed form, with no linear\-programming solve and a dual witness for re\-verification\. A linear minimisation oracle over the box can be implemented as a sort plus a prefix sum\.


### 4 The honest envelope

[Dependency view](group-04-the-honest-envelope.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The honest model is an honest envelope: a lower and an upper honest probability per outcome, whose envelope set holds every law between them\. One honest law is the degenerate envelope, and assuming that every round's conditional outcome law lies in a degenerate envelope is the same as assuming independent rounds with that law \([Lemma “The envelope assumption at a degenerate envelope”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/lem:hon-degenerate)\)\. A conservative evaluation takes the envelope, the budget and its allocation as exact rationals and decides every tail comparison exactly or against an upward\-rounded bound \([Definition “Exact data and conservative evaluation”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/def:conservative-evaluation)\)\.

- 4.1 [`statistics/def:envelope`](../claims/def-envelope.tex): Honest envelope and its envelope set ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/def:envelope))

  **Non-normative summary.** An honest envelope is a lower and an upper exact rational probability per outcome, and its envelope set holds every law in the simplex between them\. A single honest law is the degenerate envelope, whose envelope set is that law alone\.

- 4.2 [`statistics/lem:hon-degenerate`](../claims/lem-hon-degenerate.tex): The envelope assumption at a degenerate envelope ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/lem:hon-degenerate))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every round's conditional honest law lying in a degenerate envelope is the same as independent, identically distributed honest rounds with that one law\. The envelope assumption therefore generalises the single\-law assumption\.

- 4.3 [`statistics/def:conservative-evaluation`](../claims/def-conservative-evaluation.tex): Exact data and conservative evaluation ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/def:conservative-evaluation))

  **Non-normative summary.** An evaluation of the construction is conservative when the honest envelope, the completeness budget and its allocation are exact rationals and every tail comparison is decided exactly or against an upward\-rounded tail\. It is the arithmetic under which rounding can only widen the acceptance set\.

## Part III: Binomial box construction

**Non-normative summary.** This submodule turns an honest envelope, a completeness budget and its allocation over selected outcomes into a box of accepted frequencies\. An admissible endpoint rule returns, per outcome, a lower count at the envelope's lower value and an upper count at its upper value whose tails meet their budgets, and a guard widens them to contain the whole envelope interval\. Its main results are that for every such rule the construction map emits a canonical box of exact rationals that contains every law of the envelope set and meets the consuming engine's acceptance assumption \([Theorem “Properties of the constructed box”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:box-construction)\), and that an honest run whose per\-round conditional outcome law stays inside the envelope fails the test with probability at most the sum of the binomial tails at the envelope's ends beyond the guarded endpoints, which the allocation keeps within the budget \([Theorem “The completeness bound”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:completeness-bound)\); neither independent rounds nor independent outcome counts are assumed\. The construction never fails on an admissible input, so every refusal comes from the input\. Under a monotone allocation rule and an endpoint rule monotone in the allocation, which the enclosure evaluation of the exact rule is not claimed to be, a larger budget gives a smaller or equal box; for the exact rule the box is not always nested as the round count grows\. The exact binomial quantile rule is the default; the Bernstein rule gives closed\-form endpoints whose unrounded half\-width decreases strictly with the round count, and a local evaluation of the exact rule decides its tail comparisons on enclosures in log space\. Completeness is a usability guarantee, and soundness never rests on the honest\-round assumptions\.

### 7 Admissible inputs, endpoint rules and the guard

**Non-normative summary.** An admissible input fixes the alphabet, the round count, the honest envelope, the completeness budget, its allocation over the constrained outcomes, and the choice of rule and evaluation, the numbers exact\.   **Non-normative summary.** An admissible endpoint rule returns count endpoints whose binomial tails meet their budgets\. On an admissible input the lower endpoint is taken at the envelope's lower value, the upper at its upper value, and a guard widens both to contain the envelope interval\.

- 7.2 [`statistics/binomial-box/lem:guard`](../binomial-box/claims/lem-guard.tex): The honest-containment guard for every rule ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:guard))

### 8 The exact rule

[Dependency view](group-08-binomial-box-construction-the-exact-rule.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The default rule takes the extremal binomial quantiles: the largest lower count and the smallest upper count whose tails meet their budgets\.   **Non-normative summary.** The exact rule takes the largest lower count and the smallest upper count whose binomial tails meet their budgets, the lower at the envelope's lower value and the upper at its upper value\. The guard then widens this raw interval to contain the envelope interval\.

- 8.2 [`statistics/binomial-box/prop:rule-exact`](../binomial-box/claims/prop-rule-exact.tex): Properties of the exact binomial quantile rule ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/prop:rule-exact))

### 9 The construction map

### 10 Completeness

[Dependency view](group-10-binomial-box-construction-completeness.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A union bound combines the allocated tails into a bound on honest rejection, with no independence between outcome counts\. A count whose per\-round conditional success probability stays between two bounds has tails no heavier than the binomial laws at those bounds \([Lemma “Binomial dominance under bounded conditional success probabilities”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:envelope-dominance)\), so independent rounds are not needed either\. The main result, [Theorem “The completeness bound”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:completeness-bound), bounds the honest run's probability of failing the test by ε\*, the sum of those tails, and ε\* by the budget; the remarks say how ε\* is evaluated and reported, why soundness does not depend on it, and what an envelope buys and costs\.

- 10.1 [`statistics/binomial-box/lem:envelope-dominance`](../binomial-box/claims/lem-envelope-dominance.tex): Binomial dominance under bounded conditional success probabilities ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:envelope-dominance))

  **Non-normative summary.** A count of successes whose conditional success probability in each round, given the past, lies in \[a, b\] has an upper tail no heavier than the binomial law at b and a lower tail no heavier than that at a\. The box can therefore be built from binomial tails without independent rounds\.

- 10.2 [`statistics/binomial-box/thm:completeness-bound`](../binomial-box/claims/thm-completeness-bound.tex): The completeness bound ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:completeness-bound))

  **Non-normative summary.** If each honest round's conditional outcome law stays inside the envelope, an honest run fails the acceptance test with probability at most ε\*, the sum of the binomial tails at the envelope's ends beyond the box's count bounds, and ε\* is at most the completeness budget\. No independence is needed\.

- 10.3 [`statistics/binomial-box/rem:epsstar`](../binomial-box/claims/rem-epsstar.tex): Exact evaluation of ε^* and its conservative report ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/rem:epsstar))

  **Non-normative summary.** The figure ε\* is exactly computable at a rational envelope and generally below the budget, as integrality, the guard and degenerate coordinates leave budget unspent\. An upward\-rounded evaluation clamped at the budget still bounds the honest failure probability; a downward\-rounded one need not\.

- 10.4 [`statistics/binomial-box/rem:sound-indep`](../binomial-box/claims/rem-sound-indep.tex): Soundness does not depend on the honest-round assumptions ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/rem:sound-indep))

  **Non-normative summary.** The honest\-round assumptions enter only the completeness statements: if honest behaviour leaves the declared envelope, ε\* is void and aborts may be more frequent\. Keys from accepted runs are unaffected, since the acceptance set enters the security statement only as a fixed set\.

- 10.5 [`statistics/binomial-box/rem:envelope`](../binomial-box/claims/rem-envelope.tex): Benefit and cost of an honest envelope ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/rem:envelope))

  **Non-normative summary.** An envelope certifies completeness for an honest channel that drifts within and between runs, and a finite family of honest laws enters through its entrywise minimum and maximum, which is already tight\. The price is box width, which costs rate and never soundness\.


### 11 Honest containment and infeasibility

[Dependency view](group-11-binomial-box-construction-honest-containment-and-infeasibility.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The box contains every law of the envelope set for every budget and allocation, so a law of the envelope that the consuming declaration reaches gives a compatible pair and the consuming engine's infeasibility condition cannot arise \([Theorem “Honest containment and infeasibility”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:honest-containment)\)\. The same pair makes the consuming engine's minimal divergence zero, so a positive lower bound on it shows that no law of the envelope is reached \([Remark “Scope of the statements on infeasibility and minimal divergence”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/rem:hlow-consequence)\)\. The construction never fails on an admissible input, so every refusal comes from the input: a violated condition of an admissible input, or data not of the input's form\.

- 11.1 [`statistics/binomial-box/thm:honest-containment`](../binomial-box/claims/thm-honest-containment.tex): Honest containment and infeasibility ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:honest-containment))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every law of the envelope set lies in the constructed box for every budget and allocation, so a reachable one gives a compatible pair and rules out the consuming engine's infeasibility condition\. The construction never fails on an admissible input, so every refusal comes from the input\.

- 11.2 [`statistics/binomial-box/rem:hlow-consequence`](../binomial-box/claims/rem-hlow-consequence.tex): Scope of the statements on infeasibility and minimal divergence ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/rem:hlow-consequence))

  **Non-normative summary.** Under [A\-REACH](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/ass:reach) a box built from the honest envelope contains the statistics of a feasible state, which rules out an infinite value and a positive minimal divergence in the consuming engine\. The assumption is never verified here; a positive bound on that divergence refutes it\.


### 12 Monotonicity and limiting cases

[Dependency view](group-12-binomial-box-construction-monotonicity-and-limiting-cases.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Lowering the box's lower bounds and raising its upper ones gives a superset that is failed no more often and has no larger infimum, while the reverse move can break the completeness figure \([Lemma “Widening the box keeps its guarantees”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:widening)\)\. Under a monotone allocation rule and an endpoint rule monotone in the allocation, a larger budget gives a smaller or equal set and hence a larger or equal infimum for any consumer \([Theorem “Monotonicity in the budget and limiting cases”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:monotonicity-limits)\)\. A zero budget gives the whole simplex away from degenerate coordinates, a one\-letter alphabet a constant set, and for the exact rule at a fixed positive allocation the box closes in on the envelope's box at rate n^\(−1/2\) without being nested in the round count\.

- 12.1 [`statistics/binomial-box/def:monotone-rule`](../binomial-box/claims/def-monotone-rule.tex): Monotone allocation rule ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/def:monotone-rule))

  **Non-normative summary.** A monotone allocation rule allocates each completeness budget within that budget, every allocated tail budget non\-decreasing in it; the uniform rule is one\. It is the hypothesis on the allocation under which [Theorem “Monotonicity in the budget and limiting cases”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:monotonicity-limits) \(i\) compares the acceptance sets at two budgets\.

- 12.2 [`statistics/binomial-box/lem:widening`](../binomial-box/claims/lem-widening.tex): Widening the box keeps its guarantees ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:widening))

  **Non-normative summary.** Lowering the box's lower bounds and raising its upper ones gives a superset that is failed no more often and has no larger infimum, and integer endpoints so moved still meet their tail budgets\. Moving them the other way can break the tail budgets and the reported completeness figure ε\*\.

- 12.3 [`statistics/binomial-box/lem:cheb`](../binomial-box/claims/lem-cheb.tex): Chebyshev's inequality for a binomial count ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:cheb))

  **Non-normative summary.** A binomial \(n, p\) count deviates from np by at least t \> 0 with probability at most np\(1 − p\)/t²\. This gives the finite\-sample width bound of the exact rule in [Theorem “Monotonicity in the budget and limiting cases”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:monotonicity-limits) \(iv\)\.

- 12.4 [`statistics/binomial-box/thm:monotonicity-limits`](../binomial-box/claims/thm-monotonicity-limits.tex): Monotonicity in the budget and limiting cases ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:monotonicity-limits))

  **Non-normative summary.** The acceptance set shrinks as the budget grows under monotone allocation and endpoint rules, so no infimum over it, h\_low included, decreases\. Zero\-budget and one\-letter limits are explicit; at a fixed positive allocation the exact rule's set tends to the envelope's box as n^\(−1/2\), unnested in n\.

- 12.5 [`statistics/binomial-box/rem:n-nonmono`](../binomial-box/claims/rem-n-nonmono.tex): Non-monotonicity of the exact rule in the round count ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/rem:n-nonmono))

  **Non-normative summary.** The exact rule's endpoints are integer quantiles on a lattice that refines with n, so its interval can widen when n grows by one, as [Theorem “Monotonicity in the budget and limiting cases”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/thm:monotonicity-limits) \(v\) shows, while the n^\(−1/2\) width of \(iv\) stands\. A consumer may rely on monotonicity in the budget, not in the round count\.


### 13 Elementary enclosures

[Dependency view](group-13-binomial-box-construction-elementary-enclosures.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Rational bounds on logarithms from integer square roots, and two\-sided Stirling bounds on factorials with the constant enclosed by Wallis's product, are the only transcendental ingredients of the closed\-form rule and of the local evaluation\. Every bound has an explicit direction and an explicit width\.

- 13.1 [`statistics/binomial-box/lem:log-enclosure`](../binomial-box/claims/lem-log-enclosure.tex): Rational bounds on the logarithm from integer square roots ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:log-enclosure))

  **Non-normative summary.** For x \> 0 and m ≥ 1, m\(1 − x^\(−1/m\)\) ≤ ln x ≤ m\(x^\(1/m\) − 1\), with an explicit slack; for rational x and m = 2^j, j integer square roots give rational bounds of any prescribed width\. The Bernstein rule and the enclosure evaluation take every logarithm this way\.

- 13.2 [`statistics/binomial-box/lem:stirling-enclosure`](../binomial-box/claims/lem-stirling-enclosure.tex): Robbins' Stirling bounds with Wallis's product ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:stirling-enclosure))

  **Non-normative summary.** Robbins' bounds enclose ln m\! between Stirling's approximation plus 1/\(12m \+ 1\) and plus 1/\(12m\), and Wallis's partial products enclose 2π by rationals of any width\. With rational bounds on ln m and ln\(2π\) this encloses ln m\! by rationals of explicit width\.


### 14 The Bernstein rule

[Dependency view](group-14-binomial-box-construction-the-bernstein-rule.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Bernstein's tail inequality gives count endpoints in closed form, with no tail evaluated\. With upward\-rounded rational bounds the rule is admissible, monotone in the allocation when every rounding is, and its unrounded half\-width decreases strictly with the round count, though nesting of its integer\-rounded boxes in the round count is not claimed \([Proposition “The Bernstein endpoint rule”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/prop:rule-bernstein)\)\. A remark says which completeness figure a rule that evaluates no tail can report\.

- 14.1 [`statistics/binomial-box/lem:bernstein-tail`](../binomial-box/claims/lem-bernstein-tail.tex): Bernstein's inequality for a binomial count ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:bernstein-tail))

  **Non-normative summary.** Either tail of a binomial \(n, p\) count at distance t \> 0 from np has probability at most exp\(−t²/\(2\(v \+ t/3\)\)\) with v = np\(1 − p\), and every t at least a closed\-form root t\(L\) brings both below e^\(−L\)\. The Bernstein endpoint rule evaluates that root\.

- 14.2 [`statistics/binomial-box/prop:rule-bernstein`](../binomial-box/claims/prop-rule-bernstein.tex): The Bernstein endpoint rule ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/prop:rule-bernstein))

  **Non-normative summary.** Bernstein's inequality with upward\-rounded rational bounds on the logarithm and the root gives closed\-form endpoints, an admissible rule, monotone in the allocation when every rounding is\. Its unrounded half\-width falls strictly in n as n^\(−1/2\), and it is never narrower than the exact rule\.

- 14.3 [`statistics/binomial-box/rem:completeness-on-rules`](../binomial-box/claims/rem-completeness-on-rules.tex): Completeness figures on a closed-form rule ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/rem:completeness-on-rules))

  **Non-normative summary.** The tightest completeness figure this submodule certifies on any admissible rule is ε\*, but a rule that evaluates no tail can report the allocation sum or a clamped upward\-rounded tail evaluation; both bound the honest failure probability\. Neither choice changes the emitted set or soundness\.


### 15 The exact rule's local evaluation

[Dependency view](group-15-binomial-box-construction-the-exact-rule-s-local-evaluation.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A binomial tail is enclosed in log space from a window of mass points and a geometric majorant of the rest, each mass point enclosed through Stirling's bounds\. Endpoints bracketed by these enclosures form an admissible endpoint rule that relaxes the exact quantiles: equal to them where the bracket is decided, and otherwise, where the tail decays at the quantile, conservative by a number of counts bounded through the enclosure's slack and that decay \([Proposition “The enclosure evaluation of the exact rule”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/prop:bracketed-endpoints)\)\.

- 15.1 [`statistics/binomial-box/lem:pmf-enclosure`](../binomial-box/claims/lem-pmf-enclosure.tex): Rational enclosure of the logarithm of a binomial mass point ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:pmf-enclosure))

  **Non-normative summary.** Rational bounds on ln m\!, ln p and ln\(1 − p\) enclose the logarithm of a binomial mass point, with width the sum of the component widths\. On the Stirling branch no intermediate is of the size of the mass point, of p^j or of a binomial coefficient\.

- 15.2 [`statistics/binomial-box/lem:tail-majorant`](../binomial-box/claims/lem-tail-majorant.tex): The binomial tail from a window and a geometric majorant ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/lem:tail-majorant))

  **Non-normative summary.** A binomial upper tail lies between a window of exact mass points and that window plus a geometric majorant of the rest, because consecutive mass ratios decrease\. The same ratios give log\-space bounds in units of one mass point, a per\-count decay of the tail and, by reflection, the lower tail\.

- 15.3 [`statistics/binomial-box/prop:bracketed-endpoints`](../binomial-box/claims/prop-bracketed-endpoints.tex): The enclosure evaluation of the exact rule ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/prop:bracketed-endpoints))

  **Non-normative summary.** Endpoints bracketed by certified tail enclosures form an admissible rule whose box contains the exact rule's\. Each equals the exact quantile where its bracket is decided; otherwise, where the tail decays at the quantile, the excess in counts is bounded through the enclosure's slack and that decay\.

- 15.4 [`statistics/binomial-box/rem:local-evaluation`](../binomial-box/claims/rem-local-evaluation.tex): Evaluating the exact rule locally ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/rem:local-evaluation))

  **Non-normative summary.** [Proposition “The enclosure evaluation of the exact rule”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/prop:bracketed-endpoints) holds for every search, window and enclosure, so these are choices outside the proposition\. A bisection finds a bracketed endpoint in about log₂ n evaluations, and the window trades evaluation time for the last counts of the endpoint\.


### 16 Requirements: the request, its refusal and the reports

**Non-normative summary.** The engine builds the box with the endpoint rule and tail evaluation the request selects, and takes its defaults where the request is silent\. Before constructing anything it refuses an input that violates a clause its own inputs decide, reports the completeness figure its route names and never a downward\-rounded one, and records beside it the input, the rule and the evaluation it was computed under\.

  **Non-normative summary.** The engine builds the box with the endpoint rule and tail evaluation the request selects: the exact rule evaluated by its closed forms, which is the default, the enclosure evaluation of the exact rule, or the Bernstein rule\. It runs no other rule\.

- 16.2 [`statistics/binomial-box/req:request-defaults`](../binomial-box/claims/req-request-defaults.tex): Defaults for the constrained outcomes and the allocation ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/req:request-defaults))

- 16.3 [`statistics/binomial-box/req:input-refusal`](../binomial-box/claims/req-input-refusal.tex): Refusal of an input that violates a decidable clause ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/req:input-refusal))

  **Non-normative summary.** Before constructing anything the engine decides every condition of an admissible input its inputs determine; on a violation it reports the first by its code and emits no acceptance set, repairing nothing\. ### 17 Requirements: the enclosure evaluation

[Dependency view](group-17-binomial-box-construction-requirements-the-enclosure-evaluation.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** On the enclosure evaluation of the exact rule the engine finds every bracketed endpoint by one fixed, total and deterministic search, and computes in log space with directed rounding, never forming a number of the size of a mass point\. It records, per constrained outcome and side, whether the bracket was decided\.

- 17.1 [`statistics/binomial-box/req:search-procedure`](../binomial-box/claims/req-search-procedure.tex): One fixed search for the enclosure evaluation ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/req:search-procedure))

  **Non-normative summary.** On the enclosure evaluation of the exact rule the engine finds every bracketed endpoint by one fixed, total and deterministic search with fixed parameters, the procedure the interface records\. Fixing it is what makes the evaluation an endpoint rule\.

- 17.2 [`statistics/binomial-box/req:enclosure-substrate`](../binomial-box/claims/req-enclosure-substrate.tex): Log-space arithmetic with directed rounding ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/binomial-box/req:enclosure-substrate))

## Part IV: Singleton acceptance set

**Non-normative summary.** This submodule treats the acceptance test that accepts exactly one declared frequency vector\. Its main result is that under independent honest rounds with one law the test's accept probability is the multinomial probability of that vector \([Theorem “Completeness of a singleton acceptance set”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/singleton/thm:singleton-completeness)\)\. A vector off the count grid, or one that puts weight on an outcome the honest law never produces, is never accepted, and then no completeness budget below one exists; otherwise the least budget is one minus the accept probability\. That budget is zero only when the honest law and the vector are the same point mass, and at any realistic alphabet and round count it is close to one, which is why the construction map never emits a singleton\. An instance that declares one records its least budget, or that none exists, and the singleton reads the honest model in point form only\.

### 18 Completeness of a singleton acceptance set

[Dependency view](group-18-singleton-acceptance-set-completeness-of-a-singleton-acceptance-set.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A test that accepts exactly one frequency vector is far more restrictive than a box around the honest law\. The main result, [Theorem “Completeness of a singleton acceptance set”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/singleton/thm:singleton-completeness), computes its accept probability from the multinomial law, says when no completeness budget below one exists and when the least budget is zero; the remark explains why the construction map never emits a singleton\.

- 18.1 [`statistics/singleton/thm:singleton-completeness`](../singleton/claims/thm-singleton-completeness.tex): Completeness of a singleton acceptance set ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/singleton/thm:singleton-completeness))

  **Non-normative summary.** Under i\.i\.d\. honest rounds a singleton acceptance set accepts with its point's multinomial probability\. That is 0, with no completeness budget below one, if n times a coordinate is no integer or the point leaves the law's support; else the least budget is one minus it, zero only for one point mass\.

- 18.2 [`statistics/singleton/rem:singleton-honest`](../singleton/claims/rem-singleton-honest.tex): Certifiability and completeness of the singleton ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-statistics/singleton/rem:singleton-honest))

  **Non-normative summary.** The consuming engine certifies a singleton's acceptance\-side minimum, but its completeness figure 1 − Pr\[freq = q\*\] is close to one at realistic alphabets and round counts, and no budget exists whenever n q\* leaves the count grid\. Its accept probability needs one honest law\.
