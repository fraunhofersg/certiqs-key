## Module summary

Summaries explain purpose and content. They are non-normative; the claim statements, proofs and declared assumptions remain authoritative.

**Non-normative summary.** The transmitter module describes Alice's side of a protocol: what she prepares, the system she retains, and whether her source is trusted\. Its input is a source specification, with an ordered alphabet, a retained system carrying a sector structure, her measurement and the signal ensemble, declared as binary64 matrices or through a submodule's source model and connected by source links to the virtual ensemble on which the certificate is written\. In trusted\-source mode a source\-replacement certificate, declared as a pure projector or as positive sector blocks or defined from the virtual ensemble, describes the preparation as a measurement of Alice's retained system\. The module's main results are these: [Theorem “Checkability and sufficiency of the source\-replacement certificate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/thm:certificate-sufficiency) shows that the certificate's algebraic conditions are, in either declared form, finitely many exact decisions and that her measurement commutes with every attack that leaves her retained system and outcome alone, which retention and the sequential round structure make every attack of a round; [Lemma “Certificate defined by an ensemble presentation”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/lem:ensemble-presentation) constructs from the virtual ensemble, given as sector blocks, a certificate with pure blocks whose defining identities hold with no error term; and [Lemma “Matched pinching of a block certificate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/lem:matched-pinching) states what a block certificate implies for the states an attack reaches\. Every condition the module decides is decided exactly on the declared entries, while the retention of Alice's system remains an attestation that the module records and never verifies\. The module emits an artefact: the declared data with the source segment, a licence record for a verified certificate, which lets a consumer fix Alice's marginal, and an assumption ledger naming the assumptions the artefact rests on\. Its consumers are the protocol compiler, the reductions module and the key\-rate engine of meat\-core; the module itself states no key rate, builds no optimisation problem and restricts no feasible set\. Its decoy submodule declares a weak\-coherent\-pulse source and the tagging link that makes it finite\.

### 1 Source specifications and source modes

[Dependency view](group-01-source-specifications-and-source-modes.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Alice's side of a protocol is fixed by what she declares and by the mode in which she declares it\. [Definition “Source specification”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/def:source-spec) lists the declared data and the number model in which every condition is decided exactly\. [Remark “The two source modes”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/rem:source-mode) says what each mode asserts, why a declared marginal needs an attestation, and why untrusted\-source mode is always the safe choice\.

- 1.1 [`transmitter/def:source-spec`](../claims/def-source-spec.tex): Source specification ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/def:source-spec))

  **Non-normative summary.** Alice's alphabet, retained system with sectors, measurement and signal ensemble are declared, with binary64 numbers and exact decisions, or defined from declared data or a source model\. Trusted\-source mode adds a marginal and, above dimension one, a certificate; untrusted\-source mode has neither\.

- 1.2 [`transmitter/rem:source-mode`](../claims/rem-source-mode.tex): The two source modes ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/rem:source-mode))

  **Non-normative summary.** Only trusted\-source mode asserts that Alice's retained system never reaches the adversary\. ### 2 Source links and the source segment

[Dependency view](group-02-source-links-and-the-source-segment.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** A security proof may analyse a replacement of the protocol, reached by a reduction chain whose source stage, the source segment, changes what Alice emits and whose receiver stage changes the measurement\. A source link maps one Alice\-side ensemble to another with the same labels and weights, and a segment of links runs from the virtual ensemble, on which the certificate is written, to the specified one; each channel is declared data or a named theorem of a submodule, and in the second case the link carries an obligation record\. Because each link applies one channel to the transmitted system for every label, the adversary could apply it herself, so, in trusted\-source mode, security of the virtual protocol carries over to the specified one \([Lemma “Exactness of the source map in trusted\-source mode”](../../reductions/contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-reductions/lem:source-transfer)\)\. [Definition “Source segment and its interface conditions”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/def:source-segment) states which link kinds are admitted and what a consumer decides of a presented segment; the one admitted kind is the decoy submodule's tagging link \([Proposition “Tagging channel for weights at or below the Poisson weights”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/prop:tagging)\)\.

- 2.1 [`transmitter/def:source-link`](../claims/def-source-link.tex): Source link ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/def:source-link))

  **Non-normative summary.** A source link maps one Alice\-side signal ensemble to another with the same labels and weights, through a channel given as data or by a named theorem\. A theorem\-supplied channel carries an obligation record, whose registry entry a consumer decides and whose family membership it does not\.

- 2.2 [`transmitter/def:source-segment`](../claims/def-source-segment.tex): Source segment and its interface conditions ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/def:source-segment))

  **Non-normative summary.** A source segment chains source links from the virtual ensemble to the specified one, preserving labels and weights, with the source identity decided exactly for data links\. A link kind is admitted only with a soundness claim, and a consumer decides its registry entry, ledger and provenance\.

- 2.3 [`transmitter/rem:segment-scope`](../claims/rem-segment-scope.tex): Scope of the source-segment conditions ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/rem:segment-scope))

  **Non-normative summary.** The registry conditions decide that a link names a claim proved for its kind, carries that claim's assumptions and comes from the admitting module; they do not decide that the claim applies to these descriptors\. Composing the segment with the receiver segment is stated elsewhere\.


### 3 The source-replacement certificate

[Dependency view](group-03-the-source-replacement-certificate.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Source replacement describes a preparation as the measurement of a system Alice retains, so that a security proof can work with an entangled state\. The imported construction is canonical, and [Definition “Source\-replacement certificate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/def:srcert) abstracts the property that makes declared data such a description, as an operator on Alice's system and the virtual transmitted system, the system of the virtual ensemble from which the source segment produces the specified one\. It is declared as a pure projector or as positive sector blocks, or defined from the virtual ensemble alone\. The section's main result, [Lemma “Certificate defined by an ensemble presentation”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/lem:ensemble-presentation), constructs the certificate an ensemble presentation defines, so that no square root or quotient of a declared number is ever declared\. Every decided condition is decided exactly on the declared binary64 entries, in the sense of the number model of [Definition “Source specification”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/def:source-spec); the retention condition is an attestation\.

- 3.1 [`transmitter/fact:sr`](../claims/fact-sr.tex): Source replacement for prepare-and-measure protocols ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/fact:sr))

  **Non-normative summary.** Preparing labelled signals is equivalent to preparing purifications with a shield system and measuring a retained label register\. This is the entanglement\-based description on which the trusted\-source constraints of the module rest\.

- 3.2 [`transmitter/rem:import`](../claims/rem-import.tex): Scope of the imported source replacement ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/rem:import))

  **Non-normative summary.** The imported construction gives Alice's retained label register one dimension per letter, with a singular marginal whenever the signal states are linearly dependent, as in BB84\. [Theorem “Checkability and sufficiency of the source\-replacement certificate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/thm:certificate-sufficiency) removes that constraint; the import says nothing about granting the adversary a purification\.

- 3.3 [`transmitter/def:srcert`](../claims/def-srcert.tex): Source-replacement certificate ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/def:srcert))

  **Non-normative summary.** An operator on Alice's system and the virtual signal whose partial trace over the signal is her marginal and whose partial traces against her measurement elements are the weighted virtual members\. It is declared in pure or block form or defined by an ensemble presentation; retention is attested\.

- 3.4 [`transmitter/lem:ensemble-presentation`](../claims/lem-ensemble-presentation.tex): Certificate defined by an ensemble presentation ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/lem:ensemble-presentation))

  **Non-normative summary.** A virtual ensemble in sector blocks defines, by canonical purification and a square\-root measurement, a block certificate with pure blocks that exactly reproduces Alice's marginal and, under that measurement, the ensemble\. Only her marginal, a transposed sum of declared blocks, is formed as numbers\.


### 4 Sufficiency of the certificate and its scope

[Dependency view](group-04-sufficiency-of-the-certificate-and-its-scope.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The section's first main result, [Theorem “Checkability and sufficiency of the source\-replacement certificate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/thm:certificate-sufficiency), shows that the certificate's algebraic conditions are, in either declared form, finitely many exact decisions and that Alice's measurement may be moved across every attack that acts only on the transmitted system and the adversary's registers, starting from a register in a product state with the source; retention and the sequential round structure make the attack of a round such a channel\. [Remark “Necessity and cost of the outcome\-register hypothesis”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/rem:xreg) shows that the hypothesis on the attack cannot be dropped, [Corollary “Certificates beyond the canonical form”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/cor:noncanonical) that BB84 admits a certificate on a qubit, and [Remark “Purity and exact declarability of the certificate forms”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/rem:certificate-forms) what purity adds\. [Lemma “Matched pinching of a block certificate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/lem:matched-pinching), the second main result, states what a block certificate implies for the states an attack reaches; the closing remarks bound what a verified certificate buys, down to a one\-dimensional retained system\.

- 4.1 [`transmitter/thm:certificate-sufficiency`](../claims/thm-certificate-sufficiency.tex): Checkability and sufficiency of the source-replacement certificate ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/thm:certificate-sufficiency))

  **Non-normative summary.** In any form \(pure projector, sector blocks, ensemble presentation\) the certificate's algebraic conditions are finitely many exact decisions making Alice's measurement reproduce the virtual ensemble\. Under retention and sequential rounds she may measure before or after any attack: source replacement\.

- 4.2 [`transmitter/rem:xreg`](../claims/rem-xreg.tex): Necessity and cost of the outcome-register hypothesis ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/rem:xreg))

  **Non-normative summary.** Without the hypothesis that the attack leaves Alice's outcome register alone, part \(iii\) of [Theorem “Checkability and sufficiency of the source\-replacement certificate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/thm:certificate-sufficiency) is ill\-typed and false\. The restriction costs nothing, because A\-SEQ places the announcements after both parties' measurements\.

- 4.3 [`transmitter/cor:noncanonical`](../claims/cor-noncanonical.tex): Certificates beyond the canonical form ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/cor:noncanonical))

  **Non-normative summary.** The canonical source replacement satisfies the pure\-form certificate conditions, but it is not the only data that do\. Four\-state BB84 admits a pure certificate with a two\-dimensional retained system, where the canonical form needs four dimensions\.

- 4.4 [`transmitter/rem:certificate-forms`](../claims/rem-certificate-forms.tex): Purity and exact declarability of the certificate forms ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/rem:certificate-forms))

  **Non-normative summary.** Only the converse in part \(ii\) of [Theorem “Checkability and sufficiency of the source\-replacement certificate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/thm:certificate-sufficiency) depends on purity\. A certificate vector, a globally pure operator over sectors and a pure block over non\-orthogonal states can all need irrational entries, which is why the certificate is an operator with a block form and an ensemble form\.

- 4.5 [`transmitter/lem:matched-pinching`](../claims/lem-matched-pinching.tex): Matched pinching of a block certificate ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/lem:matched-pinching))

  **Non-normative summary.** A block certificate is unchanged by pinching Alice's sectors or the virtual signal's sectors paired with them, so every state an admitted attack reaches from it is block diagonal with Alice's declared marginal\. An operator merely constrained to have that marginal need not be block diagonal\.

- 4.6 [`transmitter/rem:boundary`](../claims/rem-boundary.tex): Scope of a verified certificate ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/rem:boundary))

  **Non-normative summary.** A verified certificate buys the interchange and the source identification of [Theorem “Checkability and sufficiency of the source\-replacement certificate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/thm:certificate-sufficiency) outright, and in block form the reached\-state statement of [Lemma “Matched pinching of a block certificate”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/lem:matched-pinching)\. It is no security reduction, restricts no consumer's feasible set and decides nothing about retention\.

- 4.7 [`transmitter/rem:da1`](../claims/rem-da1.tex): The one-dimensional retained system ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/rem:da1))

  **Non-normative summary.** For a one\-dimensional retained system the two source modes impose the same condition and the licence excludes no attack\. A trusted non\-empty source segment there for which no certificate exists rests on the supplier obligation A\-LINK, a case no artefact reaches\.


### 5 Assumptions

[Dependency view](group-05-assumptions.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** Three assumptions carry what declared matrices cannot establish, that the source emits the declared ensemble in every round and independently of every other round, that each round runs preparation, measurement and then announcements, and, in trusted\-source mode, that Alice's retained system never reaches the adversary\. Each is an attestation about the devices and how the protocol is run; the module records each in the artefact's assumption ledger, the list of assumptions the artefact rests on, and never verifies it\. A specification declared by the decoy submodule's source model carries that submodule's premise, [A\-WCP](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/ass:wcp), in the place of [A\-ENS](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/ass:ens)\.

- 5.1 [`transmitter/ass:ens`](../claims/ass-ens.tex): Emission of the specified ensemble ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/ass:ens))

  **Non-normative summary.** The physical source draws each round's letter independently with the declared weights and emits the product of the declared states, no round depending on another\. It ties a source specification declared as matrices to the device it describes\.

- 5.2 [`transmitter/ass:seq`](../claims/ass-seq.tex): Sequential round structure ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/ass:seq))

  **Non-normative summary.** Each round runs the delivery of its quantum systems, then every party's measurement, then the round's public messages, then sifting; each party measures in round order, post\-processing follows all rounds and every message is public\. It is the timing premise of the security analysis\.

- 5.3 [`transmitter/ass:src`](../claims/ass-src.tex): Retention of the system A in trusted-source mode ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/ass:src))

  **Non-normative summary.** In trusted\-source mode the retained system A stays in the laboratory of the party that holds it and is acted on only by that party's declared measurement, condition \(SR3\) of the certificate\. It is what lets a declared marginal delete attacks\.

### 6 Certificate and licence requirements

[Dependency view](group-06-certificate-and-licence-requirements.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** These requirements bind the engine, here the implementation of this module, which answers a request, a submitted source specification whose marginal slot may be empty, with an artefact or a refusal\. The licence is what a verified certificate with its retention attestation gives a consumer, the right to impose the declared marginal on the states it optimises over, and the licence record is its data form\. The requirements fix when a certificate is demanded, how the one\-dimensional case is served, when a certificate is refused, how a declared marginal is decided, and how the retention attestation is recorded\.

- 6.1 [`transmitter/req:licence-scope`](../claims/req-licence-scope.tex): Certificate and attestation demanded only at d_A≥2 ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/req:licence-scope))

  **Non-normative summary.** A trusted\-source request must carry the certificate, and with it the retention attestation, only when Alice's retained system has dimension at least two\. At dimension one the licensed marginal excludes no state, so demanding them there would refuse data for nothing\.

- 6.2 [`transmitter/req:marginal-fill`](../claims/req-marginal-fill.tex): The unique marginal served at d_A=1 ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/req:marginal-fill))

  **Non-normative summary.** With a one\-dimensional retained system a trusted\-source request may leave the marginal empty; the engine fills it with the only state there and records the fill\. The record keeps a filled value distinguishable from a declared one, and no other slot is ever filled\.

- 6.3 [`transmitter/req:failing-certificate`](../claims/req-failing-certificate.tex): Refusal of a certificate that fails a decided condition ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/req:failing-certificate))

  **Non-normative summary.** A request whose certificate is present and fails a condition decided of its form, pure, block or ensemble presentation, is refused at every dimension, including dimension one, where none is demanded\. A certificate that does not verify is a defect in the supplied data, not a weaker claim\.

- 6.4 [`transmitter/req:marginal-decided`](../claims/req-marginal-decided.tex): Direct decision of the declared marginal ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/req:marginal-decided))

  **Non-normative summary.** A declared marginal is checked to be a state on the declared matrix itself, even where a certificate satisfies \(SR1\)\. An implication proved of the certificate's objects is no check of the datum, so it is never used\.

- 6.5 [`transmitter/req:attestation-record`](../claims/req-attestation-record.tex): Pass-through of the retention attestation ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/req:attestation-record))

  **Non-normative summary.** Where a licence record is emitted, in trusted\-source mode at dimension at least two, the retention attestation \(SR3\) is recorded in it exactly as supplied, never verified and never asserted of a physical apparatus\. No function of the declared data decides it, so the engine can only record it\.


## Part III: Decoy-state sources and the tagging channel

**Non-normative summary.** The decoy submodule declares a phase\-randomised weak\-coherent\-pulse source and turns it into a finite virtual source that the engine can read\. The physical states live on an infinite\-dimensional two\-mode Fock space, so they are declared as a source model, with the attestation [A\-WCP](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/ass:wcp) in the place of [A\-ENS](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/ass:ens)\. Up to an entropy cutoff the photon\-number sectors stay quantum; above it and up to a source cutoff each photon number and polarisation carries one flag shared by every intensity, which ties a photon number's weight across intensities; the remaining weight sits on one tail flag per polarisation and intensity\. Its main results are [Proposition “Tagging channel for weights at or below the Poisson weights”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/prop:tagging), one channel carrying this virtual ensemble to the physical states for any sector weights at or below the Poisson weights, and [Corollary “The emitted decoy declaration”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/cor:emitted-decoy), by which the emitted declaration, an ensemble presentation, has an exact source\-replacement certificate wherever its declared blocks meet the conditions of that form, and a tagging link whose source identity holds at the emitted descriptors\. Every number is used as declared: the Poisson weights are met by a certified inequality and never by an equality, and no square root or quotient is ever declared\. The submodule states no key rate and compares none, and its emission, the step that outputs the declaration, refuses entropy cutoffs above two, quantum sectors whose signal dyads it cannot form exactly, flag families finer than the tagged declaration's and formed entries that are not binary64 numbers\.

### 7 The source model and the tagged declaration

[Dependency view](group-07-decoy-state-sources-and-the-tagging-channel-the-source-model-and-the-tagged-declaration.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The weak\-coherent\-pulse model fixes the declared labels, intensities and phase\-averaged coherent states on the two\-mode Fock space, which no finite matrix can carry\. The tagged declaration [Definition “Tagged virtual source: cutoffs, flags and sector map”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/def:tagged-source) replaces it by a finite virtual source in three bands: quantum photon\-number sectors up to the entropy cutoff; above it and up to the source cutoff, one one\-dimensional polarisation flag per photon number and polarisation, shared by all intensities; and one tail flag per polarisation and intensity for the rest\. Sharing a flag across intensities is what relates a photon number's weight at different intensities, the decoy content of the declaration\. Its sector weights are declared numbers bounded by the Poisson weights, and its sector list is read off them; [Lemma “Rational enclosure of the Poisson weights”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/lem:poisson-enclosure) encloses each Poisson weight between two rationals formed from the declared intensity, which is how that bound is decided\. [Remark “Entropy, source and receiver cutoffs”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/rem:cutoffs) separates its two cutoffs from the receiver's\.

- 7.1 [`transmitter/decoy/def:wcp-model`](../decoy/claims/def-wcp-model.tex): Phase-randomised weak-coherent-pulse source model ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/def:wcp-model))

  **Non-normative summary.** A weak\-coherent\-pulse model declares polarisations, intensities, weighted labels and the phase\-averaged coherent states on the two\-mode Fock space, as mathematical objects rather than as matrices\. It is the specified ensemble over which the tagged declaration is built\.

- 7.2 [`transmitter/decoy/def:tagged-source`](../decoy/claims/def-tagged-source.tex): Tagged virtual source: cutoffs, flags and sector map ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/def:tagged-source))

  **Non-normative summary.** A tagged declaration is a finite virtual source on the sectors a letter weights: quantum to the entropy cutoff, then to the source cutoff a flag per photon number and polarisation shared by all intensities, then a tail flag per polarisation and intensity\. Its weights are at most the Poisson ones\.

- 7.3 [`transmitter/decoy/lem:poisson-enclosure`](../decoy/claims/lem-poisson-enclosure.tex): Rational enclosure of the Poisson weights ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/lem:poisson-enclosure))

  **Non-normative summary.** For a rational intensity μ ≥ 0 and an even integer K ≥ μ, two consecutive partial sums of the exponential series, scaled by μ^n/n\!, enclose the Poisson weight between rationals\. The enclosure narrows monotonically to zero width as K grows, so the bound on the sector weights is decided exactly\.

- 7.4 [`transmitter/decoy/rem:cutoffs`](../decoy/claims/rem-cutoffs.tex): Entropy, source and receiver cutoffs ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/rem:cutoffs))

  **Non-normative summary.** The entropy cutoff bounds the quantum sectors and the source cutoff the flagged ones, and raising the second never requires raising the first\. The receiver's cutoff is a different object, which no condition of this submodule reads\.


### 8 The tagging channel

[Dependency view](group-08-decoy-state-sources-and-the-tagging-channel-the-tagging-channel.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The virtual and the physical source are linked by one channel, the tagging channel, which is fixed before a letter is drawn\. The main result of the section, [Proposition “Tagging channel for weights at or below the Poisson weights”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/prop:tagging), constructs it for any sector weights at or below the Poisson weights: it keeps each quantum sector, prepares from each polarisation flag the number state it stands for, and prepares from each tail flag the normalised remainder of the Poisson distribution\. So a declared weight need only satisfy a certified inequality, never equal a Poisson weight\. [Remark “Polarisation flags shared across intensities”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/rem:no-intensity-split) explains why a polarisation flag is shared across intensities: a finer family would still admit such a channel but would lose the relation across intensities that is the decoy content of the declaration\.

- 8.1 [`transmitter/decoy/prop:tagging`](../decoy/claims/prop-tagging.tex): Tagging channel for weights at or below the Poisson weights ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/prop:tagging))

  **Non-normative summary.** One channel, independent of the label, maps each tagged virtual state to its phase\-randomised weak coherent state whenever the declared sector weights lie at or below the Poisson weights\. The weights therefore need only a certified inequality, never an exact Poisson weight\.

- 8.2 [`transmitter/decoy/rem:no-intensity-split`](../decoy/claims/rem-no-intensity-split.tex): Polarisation flags shared across intensities ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/rem:no-intensity-split))

  **Non-normative summary.** A flag family split by intensity would still admit a label\-independent tagging channel, but it would drop the relation between one photon number's behaviour at different intensities, which is the decoy content of the declaration\. The emission therefore refuses it with a named refusal\.


### 9 The certificate and the emitted declaration

[Dependency view](group-09-decoy-state-sources-and-the-tagging-channel-the-certificate-and-the-emitted-declaration.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** The emission, the step of this submodule that outputs a tagged declaration as a decoy declaration, declares the weighted virtual blocks sector by sector and nothing on Alice's side\. [Proposition “Ensemble presentation of the tagged source”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/prop:decoy-cert) shows that these blocks satisfy the positivity and weight conditions \(E1\) and \(E2\) of an ensemble presentation, which gives an exact certificate, and computes the marginal and the numbers it forms; whether those numbers are binary64, as \(E3\) requires, is decided on each declaration and not there\. The main result of the section, [Corollary “The emitted decoy declaration”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/cor:emitted-decoy), states what the emitted declaration satisfies: an exact certificate against the tagged ensemble where its blocks meet \(E1\) to \(E3\), the source identity at the emitted descriptors as a conclusion of [Proposition “Tagging channel for weights at or below the Poisson weights”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/prop:tagging), block\-diagonal states after any attack, the entries on which exactness is decided, and that lowering a weight gives another tagged declaration\. [Remark “Consumers of the emitted decoy declaration”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/rem:decoy-consumers) says how the consuming modules receive it\.

- 9.1 [`transmitter/decoy/prop:decoy-cert`](../decoy/claims/prop-decoy-cert.tex): Ensemble presentation of the tagged source ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/prop:decoy-cert))

  **Non-normative summary.** Read sector by sector, the tagged virtual ensemble meets \(E1\) and \(E2\) of an ensemble presentation, giving an exact certificate with pure blocks whose sector marginals are transposed sums of signal dyads\. Whether the formed numbers are binary64, as \(E3\) requires, is not decided here\.

- 9.2 [`transmitter/decoy/cor:emitted-decoy`](../decoy/claims/cor-emitted-decoy.tex): The emitted decoy declaration ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/cor:emitted-decoy))

  **Non-normative summary.** The emitted decoy declaration has an exact certificate against the tagged ensemble, its source identity by the tagging channel, and block\-diagonal attacked states\. Exactness is decided on products of declared weights and formed dyad entries; lowering a weight gives another tagged declaration\.

- 9.3 [`transmitter/decoy/rem:decoy-consumers`](../decoy/claims/rem-decoy-consumers.tex): Consumers of the emitted decoy declaration ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/rem:decoy-consumers))

  **Non-normative summary.** The ensemble form of the consuming declarations reads the decoy declaration, and exactly trusted\-source mode, the rule against general constraint entries, which refuses nothing it emits, and the emission's own refusals stand in between\. It licenses no restriction of a consumer's feasible set\.


### 10 The physical-source assumption

[Dependency view](group-10-decoy-state-sources-and-the-tagging-channel-the-physical-source-assumption.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** One attestation carries the model to the device: [A\-WCP](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/ass:wcp) asserts that the physical source emits the declared phase\-randomised weak coherent states, exactly and independently in every round\. It refines [A\-ENS](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/ass:ens) and takes its place for a specification declared by this model, and an artefact emitted from a tagged declaration carries A\-WCP and not A\-ENS in its assumption ledger \([Requirement “Emission of the tagged ensemble as declared”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/req:decoy-emission)\)\. No function of the declared data decides it\. [Remark “The tagged declaration as a virtual model of the device”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/rem:wcp-virtual-model) reads it as making the tagged declaration, with its tagging link and at the declared numbers exactly, a virtual model of the device\.

- 10.1 [`transmitter/decoy/ass:wcp`](../decoy/claims/ass-wcp.tex): Emission of the declared weak-coherent-pulse states ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/ass:wcp))

  **Non-normative summary.** The physical source emits exactly the declared phase\-randomised weak coherent state of each round's letter, drawn with its declared weight, in the declared modes only and independently across rounds\. It ties the tagged declaration to the device, in the place of [A\-ENS](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/ass:ens)\.

- 10.2 [`transmitter/decoy/rem:wcp-virtual-model`](../decoy/claims/rem-wcp-virtual-model.tex): The tagged declaration as a virtual model of the device ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/rem:wcp-virtual-model))

  **Non-normative summary.** Under [A\-WCP](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/ass:wcp) the tagged declaration with its tagging link is a virtual model of the device: the states the tagging channel produces are the ones it emits\. The assumption carries no arithmetic identity, so it says nothing of a declaration whose numbers were altered\.


### 11 Emission and refusals

[Dependency view](group-11-decoy-state-sources-and-the-tagging-channel-emission-and-refusals.svg)

**Non-normative summary.** These requirements bind the engine, the implementation, where it turns a tagged declaration into a decoy declaration\. Recall the three bands: photon numbers up to the entropy cutoff keep quantum sectors, those up to the source cutoff one flag per polarisation, and the rest sits on the tail flags\. What the emission outputs is the tagged ensemble as declared, with no number altered and with the entries of its signal dyads formed by the engine\. It refuses, each with a named reason, cutoffs that are not integers or are out of order, entropy cutoffs above two, quantum sectors whose signal dyads the construction it carries does not cover, sector weights that are negative or exceed the rational lower bound it forms on their Poisson weights or are presented as equalities, flag families finer than the tagged declaration's, formed entries that are not binary64 numbers, and blocks or weight sums that fail the conditions of an ensemble presentation\.

- 11.1 [`transmitter/decoy/req:decoy-emission`](../decoy/claims/req-decoy-emission.tex): Emission of the tagged ensemble as declared ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/req:decoy-emission))

  **Non-normative summary.** What the engine emits from a tagged declaration is the decoy declaration of [Corollary “The emitted decoy declaration”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/cor:emitted-decoy) and nothing else, with every declared number unchanged\. Its ledger carries A\-WCP in the place of A\-ENS\.

- 11.2 [`transmitter/decoy/req:cutoff-order`](../decoy/claims/req-cutoff-order.tex): Order of the entropy and source cutoffs ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/req:cutoff-order))

  **Non-normative summary.** The engine refuses cutoffs unless both are integers and the entropy cutoff lies between zero and the source cutoff, the first condition of a tagged source declaration\.

- 11.3 [`transmitter/decoy/req:entropy-cutoff`](../decoy/claims/req-entropy-cutoff.tex): Entropy cutoff of at most two photons ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/req:entropy-cutoff))

  **Non-normative summary.** The engine refuses an entropy cutoff of three or more\. This is a scope limit and no consequence of the claims, which hold for every entropy cutoff; a basis with exactly declarable dyad entries is exhibited only for the polarisations H, V, \+ and −, at one and at two photons\.

- 11.4 [`transmitter/decoy/req:signal-dyads`](../decoy/claims/req-signal-dyads.tex): Signal dyads formed by the exhibited construction ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/req:signal-dyads))

  **Non-normative summary.** The engine forms the entries of the quantum\-sector signal dyads itself, by the construction of [Corollary “The emitted decoy declaration”](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/cor:emitted-decoy) \(iv\)\(b\), and takes no dyad or basis from a request\. It refuses a quantum sector above the vacuum that the construction does not cover\.

- 11.5 [`transmitter/decoy/req:sector-weight-bound`](../decoy/claims/req-sector-weight-bound.tex): Certified bound on the sector weights ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/req:sector-weight-bound))

  **Non-normative summary.** Each sector weight is decided against a rational lower bound on its Poisson weight that the engine forms from the declared intensity, and a negative weight, one above that bound, or one presented as an equality is refused\. No Poisson weight is compared for equality or at a tolerance\.

- 11.6 [`transmitter/decoy/req:shared-flags`](../decoy/claims/req-shared-flags.tex): Shared polarisation and tail flags ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/req:shared-flags))

  **Non-normative summary.** The engine refuses a flag family finer than the one the tagged declaration declares, in particular polarisation flags split by intensity\. For that split the refusal keeps the loss of the decoy content from being silent\.

- 11.7 [`transmitter/decoy/req:exact-decoy-blocks`](../decoy/claims/req-exact-decoy-blocks.tex): Binary64 entries of the emitted blocks and sector sums ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/req:exact-decoy-blocks))

  **Non-normative summary.** Every entry of an emitted block and of each sector sum must be a binary64 number, and the engine refuses rather than rounds one that is not\. Rounding would change which ensemble is declared, and so what the source identity is asserted of\.

- 11.8 [`transmitter/decoy/req:decoy-ensemble-conditions`](../decoy/claims/req-decoy-ensemble-conditions.tex): Positivity and weight sums of the emitted blocks ([PDF](../contract.pdf#nameddest=claim-transmitter/decoy/req:decoy-ensemble-conditions))

  **Non-normative summary.** The engine decides on the declared blocks that each is positive semidefinite and that a letter's block traces sum to its weight, the weights to one\. It refuses a declaration in which either fails\.
